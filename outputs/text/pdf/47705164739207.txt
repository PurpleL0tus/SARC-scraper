General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
School Information
Located in Siskiyou County and within site of Mount Shasta, the Siskiyou, Cascade and Coastal mountain ranges and including
the Klamath and Shasta Rivers, Yreka High School is located in a beautiful and geographically diverse region of Northern
California. Yreka High School is a public comprehensive four-year high school with an enrollment of approximately 600
students. Yreka serves nine feeder elementary schools including the communities of Big Springs, Delphic, Gazelle, Grenada,
Hornbrook, Klamath River, Montague, Willow Creek and Yreka. The boundaries of the student attendance area of Yreka
incorporate 1,200 square miles of a predominantly rural, agricultural area geographically isolated from large metropolitan areas.
Agriculture, government and tourism are the primary components of the local economy. Though much of the area is comprised
of families with moderate incomes, education remains a high priority. Yreka High School offers a full complement of courses
preparing students for college, vocational careers and the work force. Advanced Placement courses prepare students for the
rigor of four year universities. Career Technical Education courses offer students skills and expertise in Agriculture and Natural
Resources, Hospitality Tourism and Recreation, Health Sciences and Medical Technology, Building and Construction Trades,
Art, Media and Entertainment . Many students involve themselves with the extensive visual and performing arts program

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
The new and updated YUHSD School Site Safety Plan was reviewed and approved by the Board of Trustees on, February 15,
2023. The updated Site Safety Plan can be viewed on the YUHSD Web-site.
Yreka High School has an extremely safe campus. School Safety is stressed in all areas of the campus, especially in physical
education, laboratory and activity classes. All coaches are required to have current CPR and First Aid Training. Staff members
are trained to handle emergencies and disasters to assure student safety. Weekly site administrative meetings address safety
concerns along with quarterly safety meetings to address all the necessary protocols for our district.
Daily safety of the campus and students is consistently monitored. A campus liaison and administrative team members
supervise the grounds and parking area before school, during breaks, at lunch, and after school. Radios and cell phones are
used for communication purposes between administrators, supervisors, and maintenance crew. An all-call system can be used
in emergencies to contact all classrooms through the telephone speakers.A modernized fire alarm system has been installed
throughout the campus with an audio component to give detailed explanation of the emergency warning. Camera surveillance
of the campus is used at seven locations. School activities are supervised and well attended. Visitors are required to check in
at the front office immediately upon entering the campus area. Visitors sign in and out and are given temporary name tags
which are visible to staff. Visitors must have a specific reason to be on campus or a particular person who is being contacted.

Professional Development
The District coordinated a 2 day training and professional development prior to the first day of school to provide support for staff
going into a new school year.
Day 1 Adam Welcome was our motivational speaker. He spoke to the staff about being the best educator you can be, that kids
deserve us at our best and they need to to believe in them because too often they don't believe in themselves. He spoke to the

resources for teachers to be passionate, inspiring, the better version of themselves that they want to be. Scott Eastman spoke
and interacted with the staff about understanding different point of views of other staff members and students.
Day 2 Crisis Prevention Intervention training was mandatory for all staff who were not certified. This was an all day training
