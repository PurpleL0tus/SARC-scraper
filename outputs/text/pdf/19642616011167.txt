General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.
Internet Access Internet access is available at public libraries and other locations that are publicly
accessible (e.g., the California State Library). Access to the Internet at libraries and
public locations is generally provided on a first-come, first-served basis. Other use
restrictions may include the hours of operation, the length of time that a workstation
may be used (depending on availability), the types of software programs available
on a workstation, and the ability to print documents.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
In both 2012 and 2023, Highland Oaks School was honored as a California Distinguished School, a testament to our
unwavering commitment to educational excellence. At Highland Oaks, our mission is to enrich the learning experience for every
student, emphasizing advanced critical thinking skills as foundational to their growth. Our success is anchored in key principles:
a robust and flexible curriculum designed to cater to diverse learning needs, opportunities for students to engage in problem-
solving across all academic domains, and a faculty of dedicated lifelong learners collaborating on effective instructional
strategies. From Transitional Kindergarten to fifth grade, our approach is individualized, ensuring students from varied
backgrounds are challenged and supported.
Under the MTSS (Multi-Tiered System of Support) initiative, we focus on holistic student development. By considering the
entire child and analyzing comprehensive student data, our staff collaborates to foster academic and social growth for all.
Highland Oaks School upholds rigorous academic standards within a dynamic learning environment, supported by exceptional
resources. Our teaching professionals are not just educators; they are mentors, guiding our enthusiastic and inquisitive
students. Together with our engaged parent community, we envision an educational landscape that prioritizes academic
excellence, technological proficiency, and artistic expression, all within a nurturing child-centered ethos. Our goal is clear: we
aim to equip every student with the skills, knowledge, attitude, and values necessary to flourish as productive citizens in the
21st century. To achieve this, we have instilled a culture where student voices matter. Through daily leadership opportunities,
students develop independence and self-confidence. Our discipline philosophy centers on promoting self-respect, empathy,
problem-solving, and responsibility in a fair and just manner.
Respect, inclusivity, and kindness are the cornerstones of our school community. We embrace diversity and treat every
individual with dignity, fostering a safe, stimulating, and joyful learning environment. The partnership between parents,
teachers, and students is pivotal to our community spirit, ensuring collective support for academic and social growth. Recently,
after collaborative discussions with staff, students, and parents, we unveiled our new vision statement: "Highland Oaks - Where
Kindness, Leadership, and Student Voices Soar." This encapsulates our core values and aspirations, symbolizing our ongoing
commitment to nurturing compassionate leaders and empowering student voices to reach new heights.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
Highland Oaks School was built in 1949 and is a safe, clean environment for students, staff, and volunteers. All schools in the
Arcadia Unified School District underwent complete renovation in 1995–1996. In 2006–2007 Highland Oaks had the roof
repaired, the sound system in the multipurpose room upgraded, and the faculty lounge renovated. As a result of Measure I
passing by a strong local voter response in November 2006, Highland Oaks School was one of the first schools in the Arcadia
Unified School District to undergo major renovations beginning in the winter of 2008. The renovations included a spacious new
state-of-the-art library and media center, cutting-edge technology in each classroom to enhance interactive instruction and
learning, a new enlarged covered outdoor lunch pavilion, new restrooms, and a new student services building as well as new
flooring in every classroom and new double-paned window systems for most classrooms. The multipurpose room has new
flooring, restrooms, and new stage drapes. Most renovation projects, including landscaping and a new drainage system serving
the entire site, were completed by August 2011. In 2018, the asphalt was redone and new game lines were striped.
The Operations Supervisor, in cooperation with the principal, works with the custodial staff of two to ensure a clean and safe
school. District maintenance staff ensure that the repairs necessary to keep the school in good repair and working order are
completed in a timely manner. A work order process is used to ensure efficient service and gives high priority to emergency
repairs.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Highland Oaks School owes its remarkable success to the unwavering support and active involvement of our dedicated
parents. Throughout the school year, parent volunteers generously contribute numerous on-campus hours, playing a vital role
in our school community. We maintain an open line of communication with parents through various channels, including student-
led conferences, one-to-one teacher-parent meetings, emails, classroom newsletters and websites, the school website, social
media groups, and text threads in parents' native languages. Hybrid PTSA meetings further facilitate discussions about
classroom activities and campus events.
We offer diverse opportunities for parents to engage in our school's life, relying on their invaluable support to ensure the
seamless operation of our enrichment programs. Parents take charge of organizing and teaching the art docent program and
Foundations in Art for each classroom. They also supervise outdoor Rockin’ Recess activities, fostering a positive environment
for students to thrive. Moreover, our school boasts a performing arts committee within the PTSA organization, actively
fundraising each year to support our unique Performing Arts Program, enriching the educational experience for all students.
Those interested in volunteering can contact the school office or visit the PTSA website to get involved. Active participation of
parents is evident in our School Site Council, collaborating closely with the administration to make decisions regarding the
School Plan for Student Achievement and School Safety Plan. Parents of English Learners play a pivotal role in our English
Learner Advisory Committee (ELAC) and engage in outreach efforts to support new families joining our school community.
Room parents play a crucial role in organizing volunteers for PTSA activities, classroom projects, and field trip chaperones.
Parent volunteers sponsor cultural- and curriculum-based field trips, procure books for the library, and provide essential
resources like playground equipment and classroom computers through well-organized fundraisers. PTSA-sponsored
community events such as First Day Coffee, Hawk Walk, STEAM Night, Spring Carnival, and Family Fun Nights enhance the
sense of community at Highland Oaks School. We encourage all parents to attend student-led conferences in the fall and
spring, fostering a strong connection between home and school.

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
To prioritize the safety and well-being of our students and staff, a Comprehensive School Safety Plan has been meticulously
developed by the Safety and Disaster Committee, comprising dedicated classified staff, teachers, and the principal. This
proactive approach focuses on continuous improvement in three crucial areas: School Climate (involving students, parents, and
programs), Emergency Preparedness (for disasters and crises), and School Environment (including facilities and campus). This
comprehensive plan tackles various safety concerns, such as implementing procedures to detect and prevent bullying,
ensuring vigilant campus supervision, maintaining well-equipped disaster kits, and constantly monitoring emergency protocols.
Updated annually, the Comprehensive School Safety Plan undergoes scrutiny and approval by both the School Site Council
and the Board of Education. Furthermore, the plan is thoroughly reviewed with all staff members at the onset of each school
year.
Our commitment to safety is further demonstrated through regular safety drills. Monthly fire drills, quarterly earthquake drills,
and at least annual intruder/lockdown drills are conducted to prepare both students and staff for potential emergencies. In
addition to these drills, staff members receive ongoing training to handle various disaster scenarios effectively.
To bolster student safety, our teachers, administrators, and school proctors diligently supervise students on campus before and
after school, as well as during breaks and lunchtime. Highland Oaks Elementary School maintains a closed campus policy,
requiring permission for students to leave school grounds at any time. All visitors to the campus are strictly required to check in
at the school's main office and wear the designated identification badge throughout their visit, ensuring a secure environment
for everyone. In addition, every volunteer must present a TB clearance.
Our 2022-2023 School Safety plan was last reviewed on 5/4/23. Our 2023-2024 was reviewed by the School Site Council on
11/6/23 and presented to staff on 11/14/23.

Professional Development
Professional Development
Arcadia schools are dedicated to enhancing the professional growth of our staff through a comprehensive and diverse array of
district and site-coordinated professional development opportunities. These sessions cover a wide range of topics, including
assessments, data-driven instruction, critical thinking strategies, electronic grade book implementation, California Academic
Standards, online resource curation, pedagogical shifts, brain research, Universal Design for Learning (UDL), Multi-Tiered
Systems and Support (MTSS), Restorative Practices (RP), Leader in Me (LIM), Social Emotional Learning (SEL), Cognitively
Guided Instruction (CGI), Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS), technology integration, and innovative teaching
methods. Additionally, there is a strong focus on promoting equitable practices in education.
Traditionally, professional development has been delivered through in-district training sessions and participation in local or
regional conferences. Site-based professional development occurs during faculty meetings, early release grade level
collaboration days, and minimum days, ensuring that learning opportunities are integrated into the school calendar. To
accommodate various schedules, staff members engage in virtual professional development during the school day, after
school, or even on weekends. Moreover, we have designed self-paced modules that empower teachers to access valuable
information at their convenience.
Arcadia's innovative approach includes monthly gatherings of Small Working Arcadia Groups (SWAG), where teachers
convene seven times a year. These sessions, which were previously centered around teacher-selected topics aligned with
district initiatives, provide a platform for collaboration and ongoing professional growth among our dedicated educators
