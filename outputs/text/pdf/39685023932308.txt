General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
At Escalon High School, our mission is to ensure that all students are prepared for success in college or their chosen careers.
Guided by this mission, Escalon High School's Vision Statement sets lofty expectations for every student. We employ
innovative, student-centered instruction that is both relevant and focused on achieving tangible learning results. Our students
emerge as socially responsible individuals and effective communicators who not only value learning but also strive for personal
excellence. Upon graduation, they leave Escalon High as highly literate, independent critical thinkers equipped with the
knowledge, skills, and motivation to achieve economic independence.
Nestled in the heart of the San Joaquin Valley, Escalon is a tranquil rural community with a population of approximately 7,800
residents. The city, Spanish for "stepping stones," was incorporated in 1957. Before the formation of the Escalon Union High
School District in 1919, students commuted to Stockton schools via the Tidewater Railroad. In 1967, the district evolved into
the Escalon Unified School District, offering a comprehensive educational program that caters to the diverse needs of students
across four elementary schools, one charter school, one middle school, one high school, and a credit recovery high school.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
Situated equidistantly between the larger suburban communities of Stockton and Modesto, Escalon boasts a slow-paced,
small-town lifestyle amidst a unique rural setting. The community's rich farming history remains evident, with agriculture being
the linchpin of the local economy. Known as the "Land of Peaches and Cream," Escalon has a robust dairy sector and various
orchard and row crop operations. Against this backdrop, Escalon High draws students from a diverse range of backgrounds,
encompassing economic, social, and cultural diversity.
Close to several colleges, major Central Valley cities, the San Francisco Bay Area, the historic Mother Lode Country, and the
Sierra Nevada Mountains, Escalon provides students with unique opportunities. Just 20 minutes away from Stockton and less
than 20 minutes south of Modesto, the city is in proximity to San Joaquin Delta College, the University of the Pacific, and
Modesto Junior College. Furthermore, California State University, Stanislaus, in Turlock, is approximately 40 minutes to the
south.
Owing to its small-town character, Escalon High School is a prominent member of the community, receiving support from local
service clubs, businesses, and community organizations. This support is evident through scholarships, recognition programs,
sponsorships, and volunteering. School activities and events are major community happenings, with the EHS athletic program
and the FFA serving as continual sources of pride. The community actively engages through sports, band, and ag boosters.
Escalon High is committed to meeting the diverse needs of its students. A robust college-preparatory program offers eleven
Advanced Placement courses and various Career Technical Education (CTE) pathways in Agriculture Science, Agriculture
Mechanics, Animal Science, Floral Design, and Public Safety. Special programs, including Special Education Resource
classes, SDC classes coordinated by the San Joaquin County SELPA, and a Transition Class directed by the county, cater to
specific student requirements. English Language Development classes provide instruction, tutorials, and counseling services.
In addition to comprehensive courses, EHS strives to provide a rich array of intra- and extra-curricular offerings, encompassing
athletics, music (including band and jazz band), FFA, Academic Decathlon, Mock Trial, Interact, Key Club, and a wide variety of
other clubs. These activities are supported by parent groups, including Escalon Sports Boosters, Escalon Band Boosters, FFA
Boosters, English Language Advisory Committee, and School Site Council.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
The current facility was constructed in phases during the early to mid 1950’s and has had some upgrades over the years.
District maintenance staff works closely with the school's head custodian and administration to make repairs as needed.
Modernization construction began in the classroom buildings in March of 2014. Phase one of this modernization was completed
in January of 2015. Phase two started in the the Spring of 2017.
Solar Panel installation was completed in the spring of 2018 at 3 locations: student parking lot, soccer field, and football
practice field.
• Modernization of the Gymnasium (including the music building) began in the Spring of 2017 and completed in
October of 2017.
• New Greenhouse construction was completed during the Spring of 2020.
• Early Ag Farm facility upgrades discussion started in the Spring of 2017 and are ongoing. Ag farm construction
broke ground at the Van Allen property in August of 2017 with the installation and care of an ag farm almond
orchard. Construction blueprints of structures and the rest of the facility have been made public. Structure blueprints
were submitted to county in December of 2017. This project is currently being funded through public donations and
is ongoing.
An all weather track was installed in the fall of 2023 along with perimeter fencing and new turf for the football field.
Stadium bathrooms were upgraded with new stalls, flooring, paint, and sinks.

2022-23 Career Technical Education Programs
2022-23 Career Technical Education Programs
As defined in the district LCAP and site SPSA, EHS aims to have 100% of graduating students to have a high degree of
workability preparation. CTE pathways offered: Agricultural and Natural Resources, Public Services. The primary CTE
representative is the site principal. The CTE Advisory Committee is composed of teachers and local agriculture and first
responder businesses/agencies.
Agricultural students have the option of pursuing a range of pathways in ag welding, floral culture, ag science, and animal
science. Agriculture courses offered: Ag Mechanics I, Intermediate Ag Mechanics, Advanced Ag Mechanics, Ornamental
Floral Culture, Floral I, Floral II, Ag Earth Science, Ag Biology, Ag Chemistry, Animal Science, Vet Science, Ag Leadership. An
articulation agreement with MJC exists for the Animal Science Pathway.
Escalon High School offers two Public Services pathways in Emergency Response: EMT and Fire Science. Courses are
currently being taught by 2 instructors with industry experience. Courses offered: EMT I, EMT II, Fire Science I, Fire Science
II. EHS is partnered with Escalon Police, Escalon Ambulance, and Escalon Fire. An EMT articulation agreement has been
made with MJC. An articulation agreement application has been submitted to MJC for Fire Science.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Escalon High School encourages and values parent involvement, offering a variety of opportunities for active participation.
Boosters' clubs dedicated to FFA, Band, and Sports create avenues for parents to engage directly with the programs their
students are involved in. In addition to these specialized clubs, annual teacher conferences, Back to School Night, and Open
House events provide parents with direct access to insights about their students' educational journeys.
For parents seeking valuable information about post-high school programs and scholarships, College Night serves as an
essential resource. This event is designed to equip parents with the knowledge needed to guide their students in making
informed decisions about their future educational paths.
The School Site Council, a dynamic and engaged body, plays a crucial role in implementing new programs and facilitating the
WASC accreditation process. Beyond that, it actively contributes to enhancing communication between the school and home,
ensuring a collaborative and supportive educational environment.
To further enrich the EHS community, parents can also join the English Language Advisory Committee (ELAC). This committee
provides a platform for parents to contribute their insights and perspectives, fostering a diverse and inclusive school
community.
For those interested in committee membership and more details, information can be found at escalonusd.org or by calling (209)
838-7073. Escalon High School values the active involvement of parents and invites them to be an integral part of the school's
vibrant community.

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
The school's comprehensive safety plan undergoes an annual review, update, and approval process led by the school safety
committee and the EUSD school board. This committee comprises representatives from local law enforcement, certificated
employees, classified employees, and site administration. Regular safety inspections are conducted, and necessary corrections
are promptly addressed. Mandated fire, earthquake, and lockdown drills are carried out each semester. Before each training
session, staff and students receive plan updates during scheduled meetings. Annually, the Safety Plan is subject to board
approval.
All staff members actively contribute to fostering a positive school climate that emphasizes respect for diversity and personal
and social responsibility. This commitment is reflected in their classroom behavior management plans and interpersonal
relationships with students. Teachers communicate these expectations clearly at the beginning of the school year or when a
new student joins the class. Additionally, they convey these policies to parents through written communication and verbal
discussions at parent conferences, phone conferences, and events such as Back-to-School Night and Open House.
Collaborative relationships have been established among city government, the San Joaquin County Office of Education, local
law enforcement, the county probation and juvenile justice system, and community and church organizations. Regular meetings
with the Escalon Police Department serve as platforms for sharing information, addressing concerns, discussing potential
problems, and determining strategies to resolve safety issues. Local service clubs, including the Escalon Sunrise Rotary, Lions,
and Kiwanis clubs, as well as various churches, collaborate with the district to address issues and concerns.
In addition to classroom supervision, students receive supervision during extracurricular activities before, during, and after
instructional time. This supervision is provided by law enforcement, site staff, and a campus supervisor. School staff are
consistently updated on current issues and informed of potential problems to ensure an ongoing commitment to safety.
The curriculum places a strong emphasis on drug/alcohol prevention and intervention strategies, outlining expectations for

academically. Special programs and guest presenters are featured during Red Ribbon Week, and biannual Every Fifteen

2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Fiscal Year 2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Escalon High School continues to provide appropriate core instruction (ie Math, ELA, Science and Social Studies) and
opportunities for enrichment in a multitude of disciplines (Music, Art, Agriculture, Public Safety, Physical Education). Escalon
High School's mission is to ensure all students are college or career ready. It is the goal of Escalon High School to adequately
fund all programs with an emphasis on the CTE program in order to give students the hands-on experience they need to
effectively learn the skills needed in the workforce. Federal Perkins Grant CTE Incentive Grant, and Agriculture Incentive Grant
are used to enhance both Agriculture and Public Safety Pathways. Site/district resources are allocated to departments for
enrichment and intervention purposes. EHS teachers are highly qualified professionals with the appropriate credentials for
each content area. ELD courses are instructed by trained and qualified teachers with an increased emphasis on progress
monitoring of level 1 to level 4 students. Special Education courses are taught by qualified teachers and provided to all
students that qualify for special education services. Music, Art, and Foreign Language courses are also offered. The music
program has grown from two music course offerings to a diverse list of six course options: Jazz, Guitar, Wind Ensemble,
Orchestra, History of Music in the 20th Century, and Choir. To meet the demand of student mental health, EHS now has a full
time school psychologist for student support.--------
