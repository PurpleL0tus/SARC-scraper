General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.
Internet Access Internet access is available at public libraries and other locations that are publicly
accessible (e.g., the California State Library). Access to the Internet at libraries and
public locations is generally provided on a first-come, first-served basis. Other use
restrictions may include the hours of operation, the length of time that a workstation
may be used (depending on availability), the types of software programs available
on a workstation, and the ability to print documents.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
Principal's Message
Port of Los Angeles High School (POLAHS) is an independent, public charter school in San Pedro. Its location near the world’s
largest port complex adds to the unique character and mission of the school. POLAHS inspires and educates all students by
integrating a maritime theme into a rigorous academic curriculum, with optional pathways in Career Technical Education. In its
eighteen year history, POLAHS has achieved recognition for its success in supporting student learning. Port of Los Angeles
High School was recognized as a California Distinguished School in 2011 and as a California Gold Ribbon School in 2015. In
2018, POLAHS renewed its Charter for the 2018- 23 term and was awarded re-accreditation from the Western Association of
Schools and Colleges (WASC) for the 2018-2024 term. POLAHS’ Board of Trustees and its faculty and staff are committed to
providing the highest quality educational experience to each and every one of our students. POLAHS students are also
supported by a dedicated network of community organizations, businesses and parents. We pride ourselves in creating an
academic setting that fosters student success and opportunity for youth in the harbor area.

Statewide Assessments
(i.e., California Assessment of Student Performance and Progress [CAASPP] System
includes the Smarter Balanced Summative Assessments for students in the general
education population and the California Alternate Assessments [CAAs] for English language
arts/literacy [ELA] and mathematics given in grades three through eight and grade eleven.
Only eligible students may participate in the administration of the CAAs. CAAs items are
aligned with alternate achievement standards, which are linked with the Common Core State
Standards [CCSS] for students with the most significant cognitive disabilities).
The CAASPP System encompasses the following assessments and student participation
requirements:
1. Smarter Balanced Summative Assessments and CAAs for ELA in grades three
through eight and grade eleven.
2. Smarter Balanced Summative Assessments and CAAs for mathematics in grades
three through eight and grade eleven.
3. California Science Test (CAST) and CAAs for Science in grades five, eight, and once
in high school (i.e., grade ten, eleven, or twelve).

2022-23 Career Technical Education Programs
In the 2022-2023 school year, 245 out of 928 POLAHS students participated in the CTE program on campus. 95% of CTE
students continued their pathway in the 2023-2024 school year. The graduating seniors of 2023 boasted a 100% pathway
completion rate.
POLAHS was able to maintain the post secondary articulation agreement with El Camino College for the Residential
Construction pathway. Additionally, POLAHS was able to create an approved articulation agreement for students in the welding
pathway. Finally, POLAHS is in the process of dually enrolling photography 1 and 2 students with El Camino College.
POLAHS continues to maintain a full-time Career Counselor, who supports students and their families through enrollment,
informational workshops, trade program exploration and application processes. The Career Counselor is also the primary
representative of the CTE Advisory Committee.

POLAHS promotes parental involvement through a student /parent portal, school events, resources, parent organization
membership, surveys and committees.
Strategies to promote Parental Participation include:
• The use of Aeries Student Portal for communication method regarding grades, attendance and behavior
• The use of Parent Square for announcements, events, and fundraising
• Monthly calendar available on school’s website
• Invitations to POPS (Parents of POLAHS Students) events and volunteering opportunities
• School Events
• Open House
• Back to School Night
• Parent Conferences
• Special Education Parent Night for Incoming Freshmen
• College and Career Workshops
• School Surveys (digital and mailed)
• School Committee Meetings (Executive Council, Finance Committee, Personnel Committee, Facilities Committee,
School Safety Committee)
• School Site Council Meetings
• Board of Trustee Meetings
• Title I Meeting
• English Learner Advisory Committee
• Coffee with the Principal
• South Bay Families Connected (Mental Health)
• Care Solace (Mental Health)
Family engagement policies and procedures are jointly agreed upon through committees and parent engagement opportunities
on campus, such as the Parent of POLAHS Students committee (POPS), the School Site Council and the English Learner
Advisory Committee. Policies and procedures are shared electronically and/or printed, through the use of a Parent/Student
Handbook. Parents acknowledge and agree to the handbook by returning a signature at the start of the school year. Parents
are provided a "Beginning of the Year Packet," which includes: Academic Calendars, a-g requirements, student's schedule,
student planner and food program information.

POLAHS parents are provided optional training for Aeries, the POLAHS student/parent portal. Parents are invited to learn how
to access the program and practice the tools needed to monitor progress from home. POLAHS maintains a Data Coordinator
and a Tech Department, who provides assistance with access to school applications and school technology.
The POLAHS School Reports website page is user friendly for all visitors who aspire to learn more about POLAHS. School
reports are translated in Spanish and posted for public viewing. Parents with accommodation requests are surveyed at the start
of the year to ensure accommodations/modifications are made. Translation services for parent events is always provided.

disruption is as minimal as possible.

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
The Comprehensive School Safety Plan specifies POLAHS’ responsibilities in ensuring a safe and orderly environment. The
scope of the CSSP encompasses the Port of Los Angeles High School campus. It addresses a broad range of concerns,
including student discipline, emergencies, policies and safety related practices, and newly added, COVID practices. This
information is made known to staff and students through appropriate training and communication. The plan is updated and
board approved every 6 months.
The intent of the plan is to address all areas of school safety, in an effort to establish a safe and secure campus where they are
free from physical and psychological harm. Administration, teachers and staff are committed to maximizing school safety and to
creating a positive learning environment that emphasizes high expectations for preparedness, student conduct, responsible
behavior, and respect for others.
As required by Education Code 32282- 32289, the CSSP is to be reviewed and updated annually and subsequently submitted
for approval to the School Site Council, as well as to POLAHS’s governing board. Additionally, POLAHS’s School Safety

2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Fiscal Year 2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Special Education
POLAHS funds a comprehensive Resource Program and related services, which serves 114 students with intellectual or
developmental disabilities in the 2022-23 school year. POLAHS employs one Special Education Coordinator, six resource
teachers, and five special education aides. POLAHS also contracts with outside agencies for additional instructional aide
support, and behavioral services. POLAHS’s full-inclusion approach includes assistance by Resource teachers and
instructional aides, inside the General Education setting. Through LCFF funding, POLAHS also employs one Moderate-Severe
credentialed teacher, who supports students through individualized, modified instruction for part of the students' school day.
College and Career Center
Through the use of LCFF funding, POLAHS provides all students with a College and Career Center, equipped with technology
for college and career exploration and application processes, as well as a full-time College Counselor and full-time Career Tech
Education Counselor. Students and their families benefit from year-around support through parent and student workshops and
in-class seminars on application processes. Students are able to explore post-secondary goals through our partnership with the
local Boys and Girls Club. Students attend college exploration field trips and receive assistance with post-secondary
exploration through College and Career Counselors, as well as Boys and Girls Club College Bound and Career Bound
Programs.

Physical Health Services
POLAHS applies for Title IV funding, which is typically used for Physical Education. This funding is designed to support
activities that ensure a well-rounded education experience. POLAHS maintains a partnership with local community gyms in
order to provide access to various physical activity opportunities, despite the school's location and campus size. During the
2023-24 school year, the Physical Education Department offered Crossfit and Kickboxing curriculum as 10th grade elective,
community-based, course offerings. Other Physical Education courses include: 9th grade traditional PE classes, Weight Lifting,
and Dance. This current academic school year, we added additional course offerings such as Advanced Dance, Recreational
Sports, and Advanced Weight training.
Mental Health Services
POLAHS has taken a proactive approach to mental health and social-emotional learning(SEL) to ensure the well-being of both
students and staff. The integration of SEL into the core curriculum by teachers reflects a commitment to fostering an
emotionally supportive environment throughout the school.
The Grade Level Coordinators have been instrumental in leading professional development on SEL, underscoring a dedication
to equipping everyone with the tools needed to provide emotional support. The adoption of CASEL competencies, introduced
during the Summer Institute, serves as a comprehensive framework, ensuring a holistic approach to SEL.
POLAHS has not only embraced SEL as a core component of its educational philosophy but has also implemented a multi-
tiered approach to cater to the diverse needs of the student body.
SEL Programs/Resources
Tier I, interventions like Suicide Awareness Activities, Substance Abuse Activities, and grade-specific Tilly's SEL Workshops
were implemented. POLAHS Families Connected Virtual Teen Center and Mental Health Awareness initiatives aimed at
involving families in the process.
Tier II interventions, included "Motivation Mondays" in Credit Recovery that engaged students, indicating a targeted effort to
address individual needs. Social-emotional teacher referrals, interventions, and risk assessments highlight the school's
commitment to early identification and support. Noteworthy is the provision of targeted interventions for specific demographics,
such as low-income and English learners.
Tier III interventionists for POLAHS demonstrate a high level of individualized support, with on-campus counseling for both
individual students and groups. The engagement with Care Solace referrals further strengthens the network of support
available to students.
Within the classrooms, SEL activities take on proactive and practical form. Mood check-ins, goal setting, problem-solving,
perspective-taking, teamwork, self-efficacy measurement, journaling, as well as welcoming and closure routines, collectively
contribute to a nurturing learning environment.

Professional Development
During the 2022-23 school year, POLAHS provided professional development opportunities through LCFF funding and
Effective Educator Grant funding. Administrators were able to attend off-campus professional development opportunities
related to topics such as school leadership, finance and school safety. They continue to be supported by the Board of Trusties
and adapt their professional development each year through board meeting discussions and needs assessments. Lead
teachers continue to provide individualized pedagogical support to teachers, as well as coordinate professional development
opportunities on campus. Through the use of survey data and observation/evaluation feedback from department and grade
level coordinators, professional development needs continue to be identified and altered year to year. Lead Teachers continue
to meet weekly to discuss the needs of their teachers and staff. They attend professional development opportunities that
address their area of expertise, as well as their leadership skills in both academics and classroom management.
A Professional Development schedule is released at the start of the school year. For the 2022-23 school year, professional
development took place every Wednesday morning. The schedule includes:
General Staff Meetings
Department Meetings
Grade Level Meetings
Department Meetings
During the 2021-22 school year, POLAHS also changed the school start time to 8:30am, allowing the opportunity for one hour
of professional development time each morning, if needed.
Teachers are also encouraged to attend professional development opportunities off-campus, and meet common planning
requirements determined by department chairs. Substitute teachers are provided through LCFF funding and grant funding
related to CA State Priorities 2 and 4 (Professional Development and Pupil Achievement).
LACOE Mentor Program: Through the use of Title II funding, Port of Los Angeles High continues to financially support
beginning teachers in their efforts to clear preliminary education specialist or single-subject teaching credentials and officially
become highly qualified teachers with the Los Angeles County Office of Education’s Induction Program. Through the support of
a trained mentor, who is typically also on staff, teachers work towards mastery of the California Standards of the Teaching
Profession.
