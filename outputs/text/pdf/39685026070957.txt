General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
El Portal Middle School is part of the Escalon Unified School District, which has an overall ADA of approximately 3000
students. The school is located in the Central Valley and serves a predominantly rural/agricultural community. El Portal Middle
School began as a fifth through eighth-grade school in 1970. El Portal Middle School has served students in grades six through
eight since 1990. Students from four K-5 elementary schools feed into El Portal Middle School. Escalon Charter students (10+
students) are also embedded within the EPMS student population.
Approximately 48% of the student population at El Portal is socioeconomically disadvantaged. About 12% of the students are
English Learners; 12% have been reclassified as fluent, and just over 10% are students with disabilities. El Portal's 6th-8th
enrollment is approximately 590 during the 2022-23 school year.
El Portal Middle School is a 1:1 device school, with every student being provided with or having access to their Chromebook.
The multipurpose room serves as the cafeteria with a full-service kitchen and a stage. Additional facilities include a gymnasium
and separate locker rooms. Students attend six, fifty-three minute class periods, including an elective and P.E. class, and have

2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
The State Board of Education (SBE) has the constitutional authority to adopt textbooks for grades one through eight (Article IX,
Section 7.5 of the California Constitution). California Education Code (EC) sections 60200–60204 describe the process for the
adoption of instructional materials for kindergarten through grade eight (K–8) and mandate that submitted materials be
evaluated for consistency with the criteria and standards in the SBE’s curriculum frameworks. The Instructional Quality
Commission (IQC) is an advisory body to the SBE in the evaluation and adoption process. Instructional materials are broadly
defined as textbooks, technology-based materials, other educational materials, and tests. The SBE traditionally adopts only
basic instructional materials programs (i.e., programs designed for use by pupils and their teachers as a principal learning
resource and meet the basic organization and content requirements of a full course of study, which is generally one school year
in length).
Adoptions are conducted every eight years in the curriculum areas of English language arts, mathematics, science, history–
social science, visual and performing arts, health, and world languages. The adoption of new mathematics materials aligned to
the California Common Core State Standards was completed in 2014, with an adoption of new English language arts/English
language development materials in 2017. The adoption of new science and History-Social Science materials was completed in
the 2022-23 school year, with an adoption of new Science and History-Social Science textbooks and materials in 2023.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
El Portal Middle School's facility is effectively maintained through employment of trained custodial staff who take pride in our
school. It is also maintained through periodic safety checks made by the head custodian and administrator as well as regular
safety inspections by fire and other safety personnel. Our classrooms, gym, cafeteria, restrooms are cleaned daily and the
grounds are kept clean and well maintained. Our fire alarm systems are also regularly maintained.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
It is the belief of the El Portal Middle School community, and research indicates parental involvement is a positive factor in
improving student achievement and overall success. Ongoing, open communication, encouragement and support of parental
involvement opportunities are inherent to our established "Vision".
Several of our "Values/Collective Commitments" refer to including the El Portal community in the educational processes of our
site. These include collaboration, celebration, relationships, and a positive learning culture. Parents/guardians have many
opportunities to be involved at El Portal Middle School. They can join our active and supportive Parents' Club; attend school
events including, but not limited to, Back-to-School Night, parent conferences, athletic events, band and performing arts
performances, and Open House; volunteer to help at school events and activities; be a member of our School Site Council; visit
El Portal classrooms to observe their child; and one of the most important ways that parents/guardians can be involved at El
Portal is through on-going and open communication with their child's teachers including utilization of the Aeries parent portal,
Google classrooms, and the EPMS website.

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
El Portal Middle School strives to continue fostering respect, responsibility, and safety through effective school-wide
management strategies that address individual needs. This is accomplished through appropriate yard supervision by
administrators, teachers, campus SRO, and classified staff; working closely with Student Leadership to obtain student input;
implementing campus-wide PBIS that includes providing reinforcers for students demonstrating positive behaviors; and
involving parents/community members in activities whenever appropriate. Teacher-led committees, including "Wellness" and
"Safety," focus on student needs and how adults can provide for these needs. The staff of El Portal also promotes a positive
learning environment through implementing the EUSD Essential Instructional Practices, using effective instructional strategies,
arranging furniture for safety and student access, and open communication with parents/guardians. School safety and climate
are also promoted through appropriate maintenance and use of school facilities.--------

Professional Development
Professional Development
Professional Development is aligned with the school and District needs assessment and is focused on SPSA and LCAP goals.
Teachers receive professional development through content standard-aligned sources, including Solution Tree (PLC), HMH
(ELA), various online vendors, and English Language Development training which is standards-based and has been facilitated
through the SJCOE Professional Development Department. Teachers also receive support and tailored training through our
District Coaches. The Escalon Unified School District implements two district-wide professional development days (during the
first and sixth months of the ten-month school year) for all district teachers and paraprofessional classroom aides. During the
2020-2021 school year, the focus of these professional development days was to continue the development of the district-wide
PLC culture through the formulation of grade-level guaranteed standards based on the guidance provided by Solution Tree
experts and with the intention of providing all teachers with an opportunity to craft learning standards within their respective
content area. Classified staff participated in various training opportunities to enhance their ability to work with publisher
materials and provide needed student support. Teachers meet every other Wednesday (16 times per school year) to
collaborate with their content area colleagues in data team meetings focused on analyzing student data and making changes to
instruction focused on student outcomes. Through the PLC process, teachers use the four guiding questions to determine the
course of action across grade levels based on student achievement data.-------
