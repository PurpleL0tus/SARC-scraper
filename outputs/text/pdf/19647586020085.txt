General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.
Internet Access Internet access is available at public libraries and other locations that are publicly
accessible (e.g., the California State Library). Access to the Internet at libraries and
public locations is generally provided on a first-come, first-served basis. Other use
restrictions may include the hours of operation, the length of time that a workstation
may be used (depending on availability), the types of software programs available
on a workstation, and the ability to print documents.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
Principal Message
We welcome you to Aeolian Elementary School, home of the Mighty Aztecs. With approximately 310 students, our goal is to
provide a comprehensive academic program in language arts, mathematics, social science, science, and physical education.
Additionally, we aim to offer opportunities to build memories that will last our students a lifetime. We have an enthusiastic and
professional staff that is dedicated to our students, parents, and community. Our teachers regularly collaborate to ensure that
our academic programs are aligned to the California State Standards. We provide all students with an opportunity to know and
understand their grade-level standards. We use flexible small groups to individualize instruction to include advanced instruction
as well as support for students who need additional assistance and differentiation. Aeolian is a student-centered school and
provides a strong academic program in a safe and caring environment that embraces our culturally diverse community.
Aeolian’s vision is to have all students learn in a safe, supportive, challenging, and encouraging environment that empowers
them to become lifelong learners as well as responsible and productive individuals in the community.
The mission at Aeolian is for our community (teachers, staff, students, parents, and community members) to:
• Provide a variety of learning strategies to meet or exceed the California academic standards.
• Encourage critical thinking in all curricular areas.
• Value collaborative/reflective teaching and learning.
• Appreciate and respect the home culture of students.
• Maintain rules and procedures to ensure the physical safety and social emotional well being of every child.
• Embrace our School-wide Expectations: "The Big Three: Be Safe, Be Respectful, Be Responsible." We also
celebrate that we are PEACEmakers: Positive, Enthusiastic, Accepting, and Caring of Everyone!
Major Achievements:
• Our ELA and Math achievement as indicated by our state testing scores have steadily increased.
• We use Dojo Dollars to reward positive student behavior and a variety of student achievements are recognized at our
monthly Student of the Month Assemblies and our Trimester Awards Ceremonies.
• During our Student-of-the-Month Assemblies, we focus on recognizing students who display positive character
education.

Textbooks
We choose our textbooks from lists that have already been approved by state education officials. We have also reported
additional facts about our textbooks called for by the Williams legislation of 2004. Our district recently adopted the new Amplify
Science program K-6. Professional Development took place in the summer to support teachers in their implementation of the
new program. We also had Amplify support teachers walk through our classrooms to provide feedback and support to our
teachers after trimester 1.
Computers
Teachers each have a MacBook Pro and iPad in order to engage their students by using their Document Camera and their 24"
Monitor. Each of the Kindergarten classes have access to iPads that they access during daily work stations. Students access
academic apps, like Raz-Kids, Renaissance, ThinkCentral, and Benchmark in order to engage in higher level learning and
practice. Grades 1-6 classrooms each have a Chromebook Cart with enough Chromebooks for each student to have their own
device. Training and professional development was offered to support the teachers in implementing the technology in their
classrooms effectively. Google Classroom is being used in all classrooms Grades 2-6. Students also have access to an
iPad/Chromebook at home.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
Our school buildings are 55 to 65 years old, but some of them were modernized in the early 1990s. We have portable
classrooms throughout the district that are from three to 16 years old. We are scheduled to start the modernization process
next school year.
The restrooms are cleaned and stocked on a daily basis. We have a full-time day and a full-time night custodian. Our
custodians work closely with our maintenance staff to keep the campus clean from litter and graffiti and regularly check the
playground and equipment for safety hazards. Landscaping upkeep is provided by the district's maintenance team at all of our
sites.
More facts about the condition of our school buildings are available in an online supplement to this report called for by the
Williams legislation of 2004. What you will find is an assessment of more than a dozen aspects of our buildings: their structural
integrity, electrical systems, heating and ventilation systems, and more. The important purpose of this assessment is to
determine if our buildings and grounds are safe and in good repair. If anything needs to be repaired, this assessment identifies
it and targets a date by which we commit to make those repairs. The guidelines for this assessment were written by the Office
of Public School Construction (OPSC) and were brought about by the Williams legislation. You can look at the six-page
Facilities Inspection Tool used for the assessment on the website of the OPSC.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
At Aeolian we recognize that parent volunteers are the heart of our school and a vital element to student achievement.
Frequent communication provides parents many opportunities to be involved in, and knowledgeable about, their child’s
education. There are opportunities for parent involvement, including actively participating in parent committees such as School
Site Council (SSC), English Learners Advisory Committee (ELAC), and Parent Teacher Club (PTC). Parents often volunteer to
assist teachers on special projects and chaperone on field trips. SSC is a partnership with parents and teachers and is the
primary group that has a definite voice in the development of the school’s annual plan and is the decision-making body for how
funding is allocated. ELAC advises the principal on the program services provided to our students who are learning English.
PTC actively engages in support programs, enrichment opportunities for parents and students, and fundraising activities to help
support many of the extracurricular opportunities provided to students, including field trips and awards. We look forward to
providing our students with various events that will promote their academic, physical, and social emotional well-being. Events
such as a jogathan, festivals, assemblies, and scholastic book fairs. The money raised will be used to provide our students and
classrooms with more reading books, technology, and incentives to continue to excel.--------

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
The safety of the students and staff is a primary concern of the Los Nietos School District. Staff members monitor the school
grounds before and after school as well as during recess an lunchtime. We have a closed campus policy. Therefore, visitors
must enter the school through the main office and sign in. Once on campus, visitors sign in and receive a visitors badge to
wear throughout their stay on campus. Our discipline policy is meant to provide a safe and orderly environment for all of our
students and staff.
Safety is a combined responsibility of students, staff, parents, and district personnel. We review our safety plans annually and it
was last revised February 2023. The safety plans include disaster procedures, policies, discipline plan, and other safety
information. The safety plan is shared with the faculty and we practice fire, earthquake, and lockdown drills monthly.
Additionally, we have earthquake storage bins on site that include food, water, and emergency supplies. These bins are
inspected every year and their contents are replaced as needed. As an additional support, the district Safety Consultant and
Director of Maintenance inspect the school campus twice a year for safety hazards. If any are discovered, they are corrected
immediately. They inspect classrooms, cafeteria, staff lounge, playgrounds, and the rest rooms. The district Safety Committee
meets multiple times throughout the year to discuss safety issues and training to help mitigate safety concerns.

Professional Development
Districtwide and site professional development days on early-release Tuesdays provide teachers with specific training in core
subject areas and provides for grade level collaboration to ensure our student's needs are being met so they may meet or
exceed standards. We have emphasized the development of foundational literacy skills using "Getting Reading Right"
