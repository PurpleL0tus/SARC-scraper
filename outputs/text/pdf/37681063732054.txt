General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.
Internet Access Internet access is available at public libraries and other locations that are publicly
accessible (e.g., the California State Library). Access to the Internet at libraries and
public locations is generally provided on a first-come, first-served basis. Other use
restrictions may include the hours of operation, the length of time that a workstation
may be used (depending on availability), the types of software programs available
on a workstation, and the ability to print documents.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
Valley High School, nestled in the eastern section of Escondido and part of the Escondido Union High School District, is a
continuation high school that takes pride in being the Graduation Station. Fully accredited by the Western Association of
Schools and Colleges, Valley High is also recognized as a California Model Continuation School.
Our school community at Valley High is committed to providing a safe and respectful learning environment within the confines
of a highly supportive small school setting. We emphasize fostering a school culture that nurtures a strong sense of belonging
and personal responsibility.
Restorative practices play a central role in our approach, guiding us in building community, managing conflicts, repairing harm,
and strengthening relationships on campus. These practices contribute to creating a welcoming atmosphere, ensuring that
every student feels connected and valued. We recognize the significance of meaningful relationships in fostering student
learning, and the ongoing creation of a positive school culture remains a primary focus at Valley High School.
With a dedicated team of 17 teachers, a full-time counselor, and two full-time social workers, Valley High offers a range of
extracurricular opportunities, clubs, and engaging activities through our after-school ASSETS program and a competitive
intramural program, allowing students to proudly represent Valley High in the community.
The award-winning Responsibility Training Program (RTP) is a cornerstone of our approach. All new Grizzlies undergo this
program, which not only guides them through navigating school but also emphasizes respecting oneself and others, developing
problem-solving skills, and learning how to manage anger and conflict during the teenage years.
Valley High School adheres to the California Common Core Standards in its curriculum, maintaining the same expectations of
rigor as the comprehensive high schools in the district. Additionally, all students are enrolled in AVID to ensure continuous
support and exposure to various post-high school opportunities, including colleges and trade schools.
Our overarching vision at Valley Continuation High School is encapsulated in the phrase "Learning for All, Whatever It Takes."
We are dedicated to making connections, building relationships, and ensuring that each student reaches their full potential,
excelling academically and achieving their goals.

2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
The Escondido Union High School District places a high priority on ensuring that instructional programs are well-supported with
up-to-date textbooks, technology, and materials. A Public Hearing on September 12, 2023, reaffirmed that each school within
the district possesses sufficient and high-quality textbooks, instructional materials, and science lab equipment, aligning with the
settlement of Williams vs. the State of California.
To guarantee access to resources, all students, including English Learners, are provided with individual standards-aligned
textbooks and/or instructional materials in core subjects, as well as visual and performing arts. These resources are intended
for both classroom use and to be taken home. The district follows a well-established review cycle for the adoption of textbooks
and supplementary materials. Committees comprising teachers, administrators, and parents carefully review the materials
under consideration, and recommendations are subsequently presented to the School Board by the Instructional Program
Review Committee. Before adoption, all recommended materials are made available for parent examination at the district
office. Additionally, schools incorporate a diverse array of novels and instructional materials throughout their curriculum.

2022-23 Career Technical Education Programs
Career Technical Education Programs
In alignment with the California Department of Education, Escondido Union High School District defines career paths as a
program of study that involves a multiyear sequence of courses that integrates core academic knowledge with technical and
occupational knowledge to provide students with a pathway to postsecondary education and careers. The CTE department
collects data on the number of students moving through our local sequences. Some CTE courses, because of the time
requirements, internship element, and industry standards they meet, may be defined as a sequence within themselves.
CTE courses are found throughout our schools. A CTE concentrator is identified locally and is a student who has completed 50
percent of a planned program sequence (in hours or credits) in a state-recognized CTE sequence and is enrolled in the next
course in that sequence or has completed 50 percent of a single state-recognized multi-hour course and is enrolled in the
second half of that course. Capstone courses are defined as completers based on the length of the course, sequence, and
skill development. The CTE capstone course leads to a certificate/industry skill sheet or license if applicable. The program also
provides the student with academic, and technical knowledge, and skills through integrated academic and technical instruction.
The programs prepare students to enter current or emerging careers for further training within the career path for which there is
gainful employment. All students are provided practical experiences to pursue post-high school employment and gain
coursework that is A-G compliant.
VHS offers the following CTE pathways and courses:
Career Pathway-Concentration Class-Capstone Class
Graphic Production Technologies 1A/1B
Advanced Graphic Production Technologies 2A/2B
Students who engage in the pathways find relevance in their studies, are provided an opportunity to apply their academic and
industry skills to everyday problems, are taught to think critically, and are required to act professionally. Through the IEP
process, special education students may be scheduled into CTE courses. Students are provided the support and necessary
curriculum modification as outlined in the IEP. CTE provides extended testing time, extra assignment time, and support where
appropriate. Since our CTE courses work to integrate and support academic standards, the students will be exposed to “hands-
on” activities and participate in solving real-life problems. The capacity to serve a “significant” number of students and the
degree to which the program attains specific outcomes are assessed yearly. Completion rates, graduation rates, and
completion of a sequence are all evaluated yearly. The number of completion certificates issued, and the attainment of specific
industry skills are analyzed. Data on attendance is also collected.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Valley High School (VHS) is dedicated to creating a supportive environment for parents and students to facilitate high student
achievement. Multiple avenues are utilized to keep families informed about academic content standards and their student's
progress, with information provided in both Spanish and English. The largest parent organization, ELAC, conducts monthly
meetings alongside counselors and administrators to discuss topics such as state standards, assessments, college
opportunities, and social and emotional development. Teachers also review student progress with parents every third and sixth
week, while the academic counselor meets with students twice a quarter to monitor their progress.
Within a highly supportive school setting, VHS fosters a safe and respectful learning environment, emphasizing a strong sense
of belonging and personal responsibility through restorative practices. With a quarter system allowing students to earn 90 or
more credits in a school year, VHS actively employs restorative practices to develop a sense of community, manage conflicts,
repair harm, and build relationships on campus. The dedicated staff includes 17 teachers, a full-time bilingual counselor, two
full-time bilingual social workers, a bilingual parent liaison, and a bilingual assessment technician.
VHS provides numerous extra-curricular opportunities, student clubs, and interactive activities through the 21st Century After
School Safety and Enrichment for Teens (ASSETs) program. A competitive intramural program allows students to represent
the school in athletic competitions throughout the community. The Responsibility Training Program (RTP) guides new students
in navigating their high school journey, emphasizing respect, problem-solving skills, and conflict management.

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
Valley Continuation High School prioritizes the safety and security of its students, staff, and visitors through a comprehensive
approach to campus supervision and emergency preparedness.
Currently, the school is supported by two campus supervisors, and administrators and staff actively contribute to the
supervision of school grounds. Security and supervision are extended to all school events. VHS maintains a closed campus,
requiring all visitors to register through the Raptor Visitor Management system. An annual review of the school safety plan is
conducted, and staff receives training on safety protocols from the Escondido Police and Fire Departments.
The most recent School Safety Plan, reviewed in November 2022, includes an emergency plan for various situations. Staff
members are issued a disaster plan book, both in digital and physical formats, and undergo training on its implementation.
Regular drills, including evacuation and fire drills, are practiced throughout the year, ensuring preparedness in collaboration
with local and regional emergency response agencies.
Parents are informed about the school's safety measures, emphasizing that students may be safer at school than at home
during emergencies due to the earthquake-safe structures, on-campus firefighting equipment, and comprehensive emergency
procedures. In the event of an emergency, students are expected to follow the directions of teachers, staff, or bus drivers.
Parents are requested to refrain from calling the school during emergencies to keep phone lines open for responders, avoid
driving to school to maintain open streets for emergency vehicles, and tune in to designated radio stations for information and
directions.
The family reunion area during emergencies is specified to be at Francis Ryan Park or the Escondido High School Stadium,
depending on the situation. In alignment with the Escondido Union High School District Disaster Preparedness Plan, families
are encouraged to establish home emergency plans and preparations.

2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Fiscal Year 2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Valley High School's teachers and staff are deeply committed to ensuring the academic success of every student and fostering
a safe and productive learning environment. Recognizing the unique nature of an alternative education high school, the entire
staff is dedicated to identifying and addressing the individual needs of each student. The school has developed specialized
educational programs geared towards equipping students with the skills and tools necessary for both fostering creativity and
building a solid educational foundation.
At Valley High School, the core belief is that every student can learn, and mastery is achievable within a climate of support and
high expectations. The school places emphasis on personal safety and growth, the development of pro-social skills,
fundamental skills acquisition, integrated technology, and a standards-based curriculum. Valley High School perceives the
community as a valuable resource, considers staff as coaches and providers of support services, and views students as active
participants in both work and learning.
The general operations of the school encompass a range of areas, including salaries, benefits, services, materials, and support
for general education. Specific initiatives such as tutoring services for at-risk students or those seeking additional support,
special education offerings providing individualized instruction to students with additional learning needs, and the Targeted
Instructional Improvement Program are all integral components. This program includes staff salaries, benefits, services,
materials, and support geared towards graduation improvement and overall student success.
Valley High School is committed to addressing various aspects of student well-being, including interventions, attendance
management, and socio-emotional needs. This involves the development of systems and protocols to address student
behavior, provide emotional and mental wellness support, and enhance graduation rates by addressing chronic absenteeism.
Additionally, the school allocates resources for maintenance and operations to ensure a conducive and well-maintained
physical environment. Through these diverse efforts, Valley High School aims to create a comprehensive and supportive
educational experience for all its students.

Professional Development
PLC Structures:
The district is committed to facilitating collaborative efforts among teacher teams to enhance instructional practices within
courses of study, aiming to improve student support in key improvement areas with measurable checks for understanding. A
Teacher on Special Assignment (TOSA) plays a pivotal role in supporting teacher teams through data analysis, fostering the
development of collaborative planning that incorporates high-yield instructional practices informed by timely assessments for
learning. These practices are intended to refine pedagogy and enhance student learning outcomes.
Teams engage in the collection of common assessment data reflecting higher-order thinking skills every three and six weeks of
each quarter. During PLC meetings, teams utilize data protocols to reflect on how students met defined teaching focuses for
the week or unit. Additionally, teachers evaluate student writing results using a common rubric. The initial PLC meeting in Week
1 establishes the focus for the upcoming 6-week teaching and learning cycle, while the Week 6-8 meeting centers on the
analysis of student work, attendance, behavior, and credit completion progress. Further PLC meetings during the inquiry cycle
may occur as needed for reflection on student assessment data. District and site leads actively support and participate in these
teacher inquiry cycles, with opportunities for one day of summer planning addressing key priorities: student academic self-
monitoring, overall academic improvement, and restorative practices.
Instruction:
At VHS, staff PLC collaboration time serves as a critical platform for teacher group interaction and increased buy-in. Innovate
Ed CSI Cohorts lesson plan and design, specifically focusing on Depth of Knowledge (DOK 3) professional development, has
led to improvements based on teacher feedback and PLC lead feedback. Goals include continued enhancement of lesson plan
design, assessments, and DOK 3 tasks development, aligning with the district's Arc of Learning design. Teachers are actively
working on fostering engagement, fun, love of learning, and curiosity in lesson planning, especially targeting "at-promise"
students.
