General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.
Internet Access Internet access is available at public libraries and other locations that are publicly
accessible (e.g., the California State Library). Access to the Internet at libraries and
public locations is generally provided on a first-come, first-served basis. Other use
restrictions may include the hours of operation, the length of time that a workstation
may be used (depending on availability), the types of software programs available
on a workstation, and the ability to print documents.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
Trinidad Union School District's mission is to prepare children academically and socially by providing a nurturing school
environment with extensive enrichment opportunities to help them live responsibly and to envision and achieve their goals in
life.
Trinidad Union School District (TUSD) consists of a single, recently modernized elementary school with an enrollment of
approximately 190 students in grades TK-8. During the 2023/24 school year, Trinidad Elementary School had 11 full-time
teachers in addition to part-time certificated and classified personnel who support student achievement and exploration in
music, art, gardening, speech, resource, counseling and other programs. The district participates in the Class Size Reduction
program in grades TK-3 which guarantees an average of no more than 24 students per grade. The district also provides
instructional aides in every class for a minimum of 3 hours per day.
Trinidad Elementary School is known for its high academic standards and the California Department of Education has
recognized it as a California Distinguished School. Our students consistently perform above average on California’s mandated
standardized tests. The CA Department of Education's DataQuest shows that in the 2022-23 school year's Smarter Balanced
Assessment Consortium (SBAC) administration, 48% of TUSD's students met or exceeded the standards for English Language
Arts and 28% nearly met them. Statewide, 46.6% of students met or exceeded the standard and 22.2% nearly met it. In
mathematics, 41% of TUSD students met or exceeded the standards and 31% nearly met them. Statewide, 34.6% of students
met or exceeded the standards in mathematics and 24.1% of students nearly met them.
TUSD is committed to providing enrichment education for all students. All grades receive music instruction, and students in the
upper grades have the option of enrolling additional music classes such as steel pan band, choir, rock band, strings, and
beginning band as an elective. An artist-in-residence guides our visual arts program, assists students in all grades to build
extensive portfolios, and provides students with experience with various mediums. Drama instruction is offered to students in all
grades. All classes participate in Marine Activities and Resources Education (MARE), an annual month-long study of specific
ocean habitats developed by the Lawrence Hall of Science. Students also participate in our gardening program, which is an
extension of our nutrition, wellness, and fitness programs. Students in the upper grades (6-8) are provided elective
opportunities in photography, Spanish, cross age tutoring, culinary skills, coding, and woodworking.
Students enjoy a warm and welcoming culture led by a creative and devoted staff. Trinidad School offers a supportive
community and a very personal educational environment. Community members are encouraged to give input and feedback to

2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
Textbooks in all subject areas are consistent with state-approved materials, frameworks and content standards. The board
approves all selections submitted for adoption by our curriculum committees, and we survey staff members annually to
determine that there are sufficient textbooks and other materials for all students. Instructional materials are all teacher-selected
with our entire certificated staff working as a committee to examine publisher offerings and collaboratively select the best
program or programs.
The district is in the process of updating textbooks to be consistent with Common Core California State Standards by subject
as each adoption cycle becomes available. We use supplemental materials in all classrooms in order to have a means of
reaching many different types learning modalities and in order to make curriculum more exciting and relevant for students. All
students, including English learners, have access to their own textbooks and instructional materials, to use in class and to take
home.
The district reviewed state-approved mathematics publishers and materials for adoption. These materials and textbooks have
been in place since the 2014-15 school year.
The district reviewed state-approved English/language arts publishers and materials for adoption during 2016-17. These
materials and textbooks were adopted by our board of trustees during the 2016-17 school year and are currently being used.
The district reviewed state-approved history-social science publishers and materials for adoption during the 2018-19 school
year. These materials and textbooks were adopted by our board of trustees during the 2018-19 school year and are currently
being used.
The district reviewed state-approved science publishers and materials for adoption during the 2019-20 school year, the district
is currently piloting an approved curriculum for review and potential adoption.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
The school has 15 regular classrooms, an after-school activities building with its own kitchen, restrooms, a multipurpose room,
and gym which also serves as the cafeteria a. The counseling office and and the school library are housed in classrooms. The
athletic field features a running track with rubberized surface and playground equipment, serving both children ages 4-13 and
preschoolers ages 2-4.
Trinidad School's main facility was built during the 1950s. It has been well maintained over the years and has benefited from
modernization projects in 1996, 2009, and 2014, during which time framing, windows, doors, and emergency, roof, and fire-
detection systems were upgraded. During our most recent modernization efforts, we added a new multipurpose room and a
new after-school program facility along with new technology in every classroom for the teaching staff to incorporate into daily
instruction. The school facilities were equipped with solar panels and a system of solar energy production in February 2020.
A custodian and a cleaning service work together to clean the school daily.
One maintenance specialist manages general maintenance while also overseeing contracted crews. Together with the school
principal, the maintenance staff does an annual inspection and rating of facilities. The school also receives safety inspections
and assistance from the Humboldt County Office of Education.
Instructional aides serve at least three hours in every classroom and oversee student safety before school and during lunch
and recess breaks. The school principal and the counseling staff visit the playground whenever possible.
The school participates in the California After School Education and Safety program in which students participate in supervised
academic, fitness, and enrichment activities.
Students are explicitly taught expectations for all areas of the campus, in alignment with PBIS and MTSS frameworks. These
expectations are reinforced and directly related to the school-wide expectations of safety, respect, and responsibility. These
preventative measures establish expectations for safety and the continual supervision and reinforcement of these expectations
throughout the school day reinforce these expectations of students and adults. At a minimum, classroom aides and playground
supervisors meet quarterly to review safety expectations and best practices in playground supervision.
Visitors must check in at the school office prior to entering campus during school hours. There are signs posted throughout
campus, and this policy is reinforced by personnel.
The fire department is directly across the street from the school and administration collaborates with the fire department and
the sheriff's office to support up-to-date inspections and facilities maps.
The district has received a Deferred Maintenance Program grant for a new heating system with greater efficiency and better
classroom air quality, all classrooms were updated in 2014.
A major facilities project ($2.2 million), funded by the district’s general obligation bond, was completed during the fall of 2014.
The project included bathroom upgrades, new interior finishes, floors and cabinetry; a new after-school facility; and an
administrative-office expansion.
The school has three garden areas and a greenhouse which are maintained by the Garden Coordinator, who works with
students and volunteers. Much of this growing space is used for foods which are then incorporated in our school lunch.
Annual school facilities inspection has indicated that flooring in some classrooms continues to be a need and has been
addressed as funding has allowed.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Trinidad Union School District enjoys tremendous support from our families and local community. The Trinidad School
Educational Foundation (TSEF) promotes fundraising for the district and support for student enrichment, and our Parent-
Teacher Organization (PTO) is involved in many schoolwide activities promoting positive school climate, athletics, community
engagement and fundraising events. The School Site Council (SSC) is a forum for parents, teachers, students, and
administration to voice opinions and guide the decision-making process at the school. In addition, parents assist in the
classroom, drive on field trips, help at school events, and provide expertise in subject-area studies. Trinidad School also
collaborates with local organizations such as the Trinidad Lions Club, Trinidad Rancheria, Trinidad Civic Club, Trinidad Land
Trust, and the Trinidad Chamber of Commerce. Parents are very involved in helping to provide input on the district LCAP

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
Trinidad Union School Distirct has developed and implemented a comprehensive school safety plan to ensure the safety of our
students, to support social and emotional development and to prepare for natural disasters. This comprehensive plan is
reviewed by both the School Site Council and the School Board, and training on current practices as well as the specifics of the
plan occurs throughout the school year. These plans cover emergencies such as fires, earthquakes, intruders, and natural
disasters. Policies regarding visitors to campus are also in place. We conduct emergency drills monthly for all students, and we
update the staff is regularly on emergency procedures. We partner with community officials and law enforcement to provide
training and stay current with appropriate safety measures.
Modernization projects in 2009 and 2013-14 included many features related to seismic or other safety issues, including a new
fire-detection system and safety lights throughout the campus. We installed school-wide communication systems in 2019 which
include both automated and real-time announcements to alert and support all those on campus in an emergency. We installed
security locks on classroom doors, as well as on entries to all other student and staff facilities. The school safety plan includes
the partial use of federal and state safety monies to employ a counselor who serves individual students and leads friendship
groups and whole-class lessons on social emotional issues such as bullying. Trinidad School employs preventative measures
and restorative school disciplinary strategies to support student engagement and connection with the school and to intervene
when students present as at-risk for social or emotional challenges. Trinidad School partners with the Humboldt County Office
of Education, PBIS, North Coast Rape Crisis, and Planned Parenthood to provide support and instruction on current trends and
practices related to school safety and preventative measures that may be appropriate for our populations. Our location, directly
opposite of Town Hall, means that city police and fire department services are easily available.
The school safety plan is continuously updated by our administration and site council, most recently the school safety plan is
reviewed, updated and approved by our School Site Council each year. The District is partnered with a consortium through
Humboldt County Office of Education and North Coast Schools Insurance Group to bring our School Safety Plan up to date

2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Fiscal Year 2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Trinidad School offers social/behavioral counseling services; a Title I teacher and Title I instructional aide; a speech and
language specialist; a resource teacher; a reading and math intervention teacher; After School Education and Safety (ASES)
program; and instructional aides in every classroom. The reading and math intervention teacher and aides serve identified
students in pull-out and push-in small group instruction. All TK-3 students participate in the Class Size Reduction program
which guarantees an average of 24 students or less per class. The School Site Council annually revises and approves all
categorical funding and programs and updates district eligibility guidelines for Title I services.
Trinidad School does its best to balance academics and enrichment programs in the educational lives of our students.
Enrichment programs contribute to well rounded students who are able to achieve success in the core curriculum (language
arts, mathematics, history, and the sciences). The board of trustees, staff, and parents value extensive enrichment
opportunities for all students, including an artist-in-residence, drama, music, and gardening in our on-site garden and
greenhouse. The school also provides after school sports teams who play in the local scholastic league, an after school
childcare program, drama, and a technology coordinator. The school prides itself in an exemplary nutrition program with an on-
site kitchen serving high-quality homemade foods utilizing produce from our organic garden whenever possible. District staff
and volunteers interweave nutrition education with fitness and gardening opportunities for all students.
The district has continued our marine education program for over two decades. MARE, a curriculum designed by the Lawrence
Hall of Science, provides an in-depth study of the marine environment, allowing students to focus on one aspect of the ocean
each year (sandy shores, wetlands, coral reefs, etc.) as they progress through the grades. This program further benefits
students through district collaboration with Humboldt State University Marine Laboratory and the Bureau of Land Management
National Coastal Monument program.

Professional Development
Professional Development
Certificated teachers and classified staff attend county presented and district sponsored professional development in the areas
of curriculum, instruction, behavior, trauma informed practices, and restorative practices. Certificated and classified staff
including after-school and enrichment personnel attend weekend and summer workshops and annual conferences, which the
district often funds, and promote research based and current practices relevant to their specific disciplines. Relevant faculty
members attend midyear workshops provided by the Humboldt County Office of Education (HCOE) or out-of-area entities.
These faculty members then in-service our certificated staff at weekly collaboration meetings. Classroom aides attend monthly
trainings focusing on curriculum and social emotional instruction.
Three days per year are devoted to staff inservice—including topics such as marine science, social emotional and character
education, instruction, and technology. The Superintendent/Principal regularly observes, does walk-throughs and evaluates
teachers, and new teachers receive local support both through in-class coaching and participation in HCOE services for new
teachers (North Coast School of Education Beginning Teacher Program).
Staff also participate in professional development during Monday staff meetings and after school trainings. Classified staff
receive monthly training during in-school meetings.
