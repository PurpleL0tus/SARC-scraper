General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.
Internet Access Internet access is available at public libraries and other locations that are publicly
accessible (e.g., the California State Library). Access to the Internet at libraries and
public locations is generally provided on a first-come, first-served basis. Other use
restrictions may include the hours of operation, the length of time that a workstation
may be used (depending on availability), the types of software programs available
on a workstation, and the ability to print documents.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
Principals Message-
The School Accountability Report Card, issued by local school boards for every school in the state, provides parents and the
community with a variety of information about the school, its resources, its successes, and the areas in which improvements
are needed. The information contained in this report card (SARC) is for the 2023-2024 school year. As you read through this
report for Cayucos Elementary School, I believe you will find that our school has a solid record of achievement, a faculty that is
professionally skilled and personally committed to meeting the learning needs of students, a student body that is motivated to
perform well, and an amazingly supportive parent/community. You may request additional information regarding the SARC by
calling the school office.
School Description and Mission Statement
Cayucos Elementary School is located on a hill overlooking the Pacific Ocean about half way between San Francisco and Los
Angeles. We currently serve 181 students in transitional kindergarten through eighth grade. There is great community support
for the programs offered. Students not only receive academic instruction in the common core state standards, but have
regularly scheduled classroom time in physical education, music, art, drama and health which are taught by credentialed
teachers and our school nurse. Each grade level has technology integrated in a grade-appropriate approach. Our 1st through
8th grades have 1:1 Chromebook programs and our TK/Kindergarten grades have 1:1 iPads coupled with a variety of other
devices available to support instruction. Our middle school elective program offers college and career focused electives with an
emphasis on project based learning. Included in our elective schedule are the following: Lego Engineering, Electronic Music
Production, Jewelry Making, Cooking, Architectural Design and Painting, Floral Design, Electronic Communication and Coding,
and Aeronautical Construction. This robust list of electives complements our Career Technical Education Pathway: Arts,
Media, and Entertainment. In this pathway we offer Drama, Stagecraft, and Lightening/Sound Design.
Cayucos Elementary School District includes families and the community to support individual potential and guide students to
become critical thinkers in a global culture. In our safe, healthy, caring, and interactive environment, students are taught to
respect themselves, others, and the planet. At Cayucos Elementary School every student is welcomed inspired, and
challenged. This past year we relaunched our behavior expectations through our explicit teaching of our PBIS expectations:
Safe, Responsible, Respectful, and Kind.

2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
Cayucos Elementary School sets a high priority on ensuring there are sufficient State approved Instructional materials available
for all students. Parents are encouraged to participate in the textbook/Instructional Material review process by previewing
materials and making comments to the adoption committee when new adoption occurs. The English/Language Arts textbook
series was reviewed and piloted in 2016-2017 for 2017-2018 adoption. A review of the Social Studies Curriculum took place
for grades Kindergarten through eighth grade. The new Kindergarten - fifth grade Social Studies curriculum was adopted at the
beginning of the 2019-2020 school year. The Middle School adoption of the Social Studies curriculum took place at the end of
2019/2020 school year. 2020-21, Cayucos Elementary School has partially piloted Science curriculum options to align with
NGSS for Middle School. School closure due to Covid-19 has delayed the final adoption choice. The next adoption cycle will
include Mathematics both Science. Our goal for Science adoption is 2023-24. We are currently reviewing science materials for
adoption in May 2024.
In addition to standard State-approved textbooks, Cayucos Elementary School students have access to many other
instructional/curricular support materials. Our school's library is well-supplied, and there is an ongoing program to purchase
new books regularly. All purchases are carefully considered in terms of how well they will support our total instructional
program and meet the needs of our students. Textbook selection involves a committee of teachers who engage in reviewing
potential resources to determine alignment with the state-approved instructional framework for each content area. Resources
that best meet alignment criteria are selected to be piloted in classrooms. Data is collected and evaluated, and the committee
makes recommendations to the Board of Education for approval.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
Pride is evidenced on our campus by how clean and maintained students and staff are keeping the grounds. The passage of
our bond in fall 2005 allowed the district to build a new school. The Maintenance and Operations Department has done an
exceptional job of maintaining the buildings and grounds proactively repairing and replacing equipment as needed. At this time,
all classrooms have high speed Internet access, we have a multi-purpose gymnasium, a music room with stages, and only one
portable classroom. Our library has over 4,000 texts, and is continually updated and includes a Maker Space area.
Construction of a new classroom building took place to better house our Resource programs, School Psychologist, Counselors
and large group meetings. Completed August 2022, delayed from original completion date of April 2022.
All after-hours custodial and maintenance is completed by contracted service providers. Staff and students contribute
significantly to the cleanliness of our campus through education and awareness programs. We continue to work towards a zero
waste goal in our cafeteria. Solar Panels have been placed at the school bus barn. School classrooms and the office building
have been updated with efficient lighting systems. Everyone is proud of our clean and safe school.
The data used to make the determination of the school being in good repair comes from the completed Interim Evaluation
Instrument (FIT). Included are the dates of the school site inspection, the Interim Evaluation Instrument completion date, and
the date of any remedial action taken or planned. San Luis Obispo County SIPE performs a yearly walkthrough inspection of
the campus both indoors and outdoors. Additional information about the condition of the school's facilities may be obtained by
speaking with the school principal.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Cayucos Elementary School encourages parents and community members to be active participants in all aspects of school.
Through our PTA, School Site Council (SSC)/LCAP Committee, Cayucos Educational Foundation, and service organizations,
such as, Cayucos Rotary Club, Lions/Lioness Clubs, Cayucos Garden Club and Cayucos Seniors, there are many
opportunities to be active supporters of our students and school. Our PTA Committee allows parents to be directly involved in
providing students with activities and programs in many areas, such as art, drama, music, classroom volunteers, aquatic safety,
physical education and field trips. Stakeholder groups organize and provide numerous volunteers for classrooms, special
activities, and events throughout the year. Students are supported through leadership training, Friday Night Live activities,
service learning, assemblies, and positive school climate activities. The Cayucos Educational Foundation is a nonprofit
organization, which supports all non-fully funded aspects of student educational needs such as technology and the arts. Our
service organizations provide volunteer time, funding, and/or facilities for student support at Cayucos Elementary School. We
are now bringing back volunteers and field trips, as allowed via the health department guidelines. For more information about
parent involvement contact Jen Gaviola, Superintendent, at (805) 995-3694.--------

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
Cayucos Elementary School is committed to providing students with a safe learning environment. A School Safety Committee
meets regularly to review safety issues and recommend improvements. The committee is comprised of a parent representative,
a teacher, the school maintenance staff, the nurse and the principal. Safety in-service training and assemblies for both the staff
and students are planned throughout the year. The School Board, SSC, PTA, and local government agencies are annually
presented with the reviewed Safety Plan. A variety of types of safety drills are conducted monthly. Our students feel safe and
understand the school supports their safety at all times. School vandalism and crime is reported monthly to the State of
California. School vandalism and crime at Cayucos Elementary School are extremely low. Our Character Counts, Buddy
programs and counseling opportunities improve the ability of our students to discuss and solve peer issues and conflicts.
The Safety plan includes school procedures for emergencies within and outside of our classrooms and school. Cayucos
Elementary School is a closed campus and requires all visitors to sign in at the front office before entering the main campus.
Emergency drills are held each month on a rotating basis for fire, earthquakes, intruders and preparation for other disasters.
All staff is responsible for the safety of the students. Faculty members and Instructional Assistants are on duty prior to and at
the end of the school day as well as during all break times. A student/parent handbook is provide to all families at the
beginning of each school year and reviews expectations, rules and procedures. The main topics within the handbook are
reviewed with students on campus during class and at assemblies.

2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Fiscal Year 2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Programs and supplemental services are provided by the school and local groups for a wide variety of students and student
activities. Some may be provided based on need while others can be earned through good citizenship, academics and
character building. Among the school site-sponsored activities are Associated Student Body, Athletics, Interact Club, Friday
Night Live, Red Ribbon Week, Character Counts, Fabulous Falcons, Golden Falcon, and the GREAT program sponsored by
the County Sheriff's department. We have also implemented PBIs in the 2023-24 school year and beginning in January 2024
we will be training in the Olweus Anti-bullying program.
Meeting the needs of all students at Cayucos Elementary School is a priority. Programs that support student learning include
Special Education, Counselors, Reading and Mathematics Intervention, Title 1, SIPPS, Achieve3000, and Smarty Ants which
support English Language Learners and students with academic and social-emotional challenges. Additional services are
contracted through the County Office of Education or neighboring Districts: psychologist, Speech Therapist, vision specialist,
and occupational and physical therapists. Support services include part-time school nurse, speech therapy, hearing, vision,
dental, and scoliosis screening. Students with special needs have access to the Special Education program. Services are
provided at our school site. Students may attend schools in San Luis Obispo County as determined by their specific needs. In
the 2023-24 school year, we are offering counseling services on a daily basis at our school.

Professional Development
Professional Development
Professional development opportunities for teachers are made available through the district, outside resources, the County
Office of Education, and conferences. The district takes full advantage of the minimum days and staff development time
allocated in the state budget. During minimum days and staff development time, classified staff also receive training in areas of
student safety and student instruction/intervention. Individual teachers work with the principal to set teacher's improvement
goals. Teacher-principal interaction is cooperative and positive. We continue to have professional development during staff
meetings and release time. Teachers are involved in professional development tailored to their particular curricular area with a
focus on providing support for all students. Early release days (once per week) enable the staff to meet together and
collaborate as one group, as elementary and middle school groups and participate in different committee groups on campus.
For beginning teachers the Teacher Induction Program hosted by the San Luis Obispo County Office of Education, which is
negotiated with the certificated (teaching) staff, is used to assist teachers with best practices. This enhances our current
evaluation, training, support processes, and staff development. A large portion of the professional development time is currently
focused on technology integration and social/emotional learning. Teachers are encouraged to attend professional development
opportunities that most meet their needs both during and outside of school time. Teachers request the principal and are offered
assistance with making arrangements. The principal shares information regarding professional development opportunities as
available. In the 2023-24 school year, the following professional development was attended by Cayucos Teachers: Thinking
Maps, Get Your Teach On, CA. Science Conference, CA. Math Symposium, and Restorative Practices. In addition to these
teacher based opportunities, classified staff has professional development in the following areas: early literacy, restorative
cirlces, comprehension, and behavior management.
