General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
Natomas Charter School Overview
Authorized in 1993, Natomas Charter School (NCS) is the 19th charter to be numbered in the state of California. NCS was a
school created from “the ground up” to serve a diverse number of students in the Natomas Unified School District and the
surrounding Sacramento area. During its 30 years of operations, NCS has grown from 80 students to nearly 1,900 students in
five innovative academies that span TK-12th grades. All NCS academies provide students the opportunity to develop unique
interests, uncover hidden talents, experience satisfaction in accomplishments, gain a sense of responsibility, and pursue
education as a way of life through educational programs tailored to their own specific needs. Today, Natomas Charter School is
considered one of the most reputable public charter schools in California and has distinguished itself both at the state and
national levels.
All NCS Academies have the following distinguishing characteristics as established in the NCS charter:
** High Academic Expectations ** Active "Real-Life" Learning ** Parental Involvement
** Differential Academic Plans ** Community Involvement

2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
The curriculum for Natomas Charter School consists of the concepts, content goals, and skills instruction reflected in the
California State Standards and Frameworks in language arts, science, math, social science, world language, visual and
performing arts, technology and physical education. The context in which these skills are developed are cultivated through an
array of teacher-designed curricula and purchased instructional materials, along with a variety of instructional delivery methods.
Students are challenged as active learners, and the curriculum encourages discovery and addresses students with a variety of
skill levels.
Natomas Charter School has made significant investments in ensuring all students have access to textbooks and instructional
materials. These investments have included purchasing Chromebooks for all students to access on site and at home if needed,
purchasing digital subscriptions to instructional materials, implementing video conferencing services, along with take-home
print materials. The school continues to partner with families to ensure students have internet access at home and access to all
instructional materials in the event of extended illness and other absences through short-term independent study.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
Natomas Charter School has three campuses located in different neighborhoods in Natomas. Both the Blackrock and Star
Academy campuses are owned by Natomas Unified School District and Natomas Charter School partners with the district to
ensure these campuses are modern, well-maintained, and clean. Over the past twenty years, the school and district have
worked together to design, modernize, and update these campuses through a series of financial projects including charter
school facility grants and community school bonds. Natomas Charter School pays a 2% oversight and facilities use fee
annually to ensure these campuses are maintained. The school's third campus, the PACT academy campus, is owned by
Natomas Charter School and the school independently maintains this campus.
Natomas Charter School has been on its current Blackrock campus located in the Natomas Park neighborhood since
September 2000. This campus houses the majority of the school's middle and high school student populations in three of the
school's academies - Leading Edge Academy (LE), Performing and Fine Arts Academy (PFAA), and Early College Academy
(ECA). Several modifications have been made to the campus over the past two decades, including the addition of multiple
buildings and structures to the campus to enhance the environment and learning opportunities for students. These include
several more modular classroom buildings, additional science labs for middle school students, a small arts gallery, an
additional workspace and classroom space for ECA, a large covered outdoor eating area next to the cafeteria, a state-of-the-art
performing arts theatre, and a school gymnasium. A new set of middle and high school classrooms, a physical education room,
a renovated student services space, and a new set of music classrooms. Additional gating to increase student safety were
added to this site through various financial and operational partnerships between Natomas USD and Natomas Charter School.
Natomas Charter School's site-based TK-5 program, Star Academy, is located on campus in the Natomas Crossing
neighborhood, approximately 2.2 miles from the Blackrock campus. The Star Academy campus was built specifically for the
academy during the 2016-2017 school year. This campus includes a multipurpose room, school library and library media
center, administration building, grade-level common "hub" spaces and extensive playground space.
The PACT program is located in a commercial building in the Natomas Pointe Plaza approximately 2.0 miles from the NCS
Blackrock campus. This building was purchased and developed by Natomas Charter School specifically for the PACT program
during the 2019-2020 school year. While the campus is a still relatively new building, Natomas Charter School allocates funds
to ensure the deferred maintenance for this facility.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Natomas Charter School is founded on the belief that active parental involvement in the school is a key component to student
success. Because the charter’s mission is to provide unique learning experiences to its students, the school encourages
families to be active partners in the educational process through many activities including volunteering in classrooms,
chaperoning field trips and school events, as well as supporting students with their homework and classwork outside of school.
Active volunteering is one the school's strengths. Families in Star Academy, Leading Edge, PFAA, and ECA are encouraged to
volunteer 30 hours each school year and families in PACT are encouraged to volunteer 10 hours per school year in addition to
their homeschooling duties. In most school years, families contribute over 20,000 documented volunteer hours. All academies
share that actual volunteer hours exceeded the numbers reported because not all parents “log” their hours with the school.
Parent representatives from the NCS Academies sit as voting members of the Natomas Charter Board of Directors. Parents
are engaged in additional committees such as the Inclusive Education Parent Committee and the English Learner Advisory
Committee (ELAC).

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Across all five academies, families are engaged through both formal and informal partnership groups with the school. These
include the academy parent organizations, Star Academy Network of Volunteers at Star (NOVAS), Leading Edge Active
Parents (LEAP), PFAA Parents for the Arts (P4A), and ECA Parent Involvement Committee (EPIC). At PACT, due to the nature
of home-schooling, parents are directly involved in the daily learning activities of their children. PACT families have an online
social media group, KOURT, where families can share ideas for learning experiences, set up social events such as park
meetups and outings, solicit advice from other homeschooling families, and receive information about PACT-sponsored events
and activities. At ECA, families are encouraged to attend monthly advisor meetings and regularly visit the campus as part of a
welcome orientation, coffee with the principal, and community breakfast events.
Regular email updates along with the school's learning management system (Schoology) provide parents access to all class
content and individual information regarding student academic progress. Additionally, the family partnership groups have
developed email lists to inform each other about school activities and parent participation events, such as parent and class
meetings. Parents are invited to attend quarterly English Learner Advisory Committee (ELAC) to discuss supporting their
students, share feedback, and learn about the State Seal of Biliteracy. Typically, many parents can be located on campus
during any given day at Natomas Charter School, assisting the office, working in the classroom, directing traffic, monitoring the
campus and/or coordinating or setting up school events. All involved in the school believe active participation by the parents of
students in all five academies is an integral element in the growth and health of Natomas Charter School. Over the years, NCS
parents have been active partners with the administration, staff and students in helping to make the school what it is today.
Parent support is reflected in the strong academic progress students are making in benchmark assessments and course
grades throughout the school year. Any parents who need additional support or are interested in taking a more active role in
their student's education are encouraged to contact their student's classroom teacher and academy principal. Current email
addresses and contact information can be found in our school staff directories posted at https://www.natomascharter.org

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
Natomas Charter School's Comprehensive Safety Plan is continuously updated as needed and follows the recommendations
outlined in California Education Code Sections 32280-32289, which provide the key elements of the plan. The Comprehensive
Safety Plan is reviewed and updated annually by a School Safety Committee. The School Safety Committee consists of
teachers, staff, administrators, parents, and students representing each of the school's five academies. The Comprehensive
Safety Plan was last reviewed and approved by the Natomas Charter School Board at its February 2023 Meeting and will be
reviewed in February 2024. The School Safety Committee regularly meets with students, staff, and faculty to review all
elements of the safety plan. All staff and faculty receive formal training on the school safety plan at least annually. Our current
safety plan can be found at https://www.natomascharter.org/accountability

Professional Development
Professional Development
Since its inception, Natomas Charter School (NCS) has prided itself on being a “teacher-initiated” charter in which teachers
take on the responsibility and accountability for creating and directing curriculum in their own classrooms to best meet student
needs. In fact, each academy was purposefully created to be a small learning community for students as well as teachers.
Moreover, teachers from each academy also have access to the collective materials, resources and curriculum from teachers
in other academies. From the 2019-20 through 2022-23 school years, professional development has focused on Multi-Tiered
Systems of Support (MTSS) by creating structures and building skills for all instructional staff in the areas of academic support
and differentiation, social-emotional learning, and behavioral support strategies. During the 2021-22 school year, this focus on
MTSS transitioned to examining student progress through deepening our understanding of Professional Learning Communities
(PLCs). Teachers and staff are also engaged in an online MTSS course provided through the Orange County Office of
Education as part of a MTSS Model School Grant NCS received in May 2021. This online course provides training and
materials to assist all staff in developing skills to support our diverse student population with a focus on Universal Design for
Learning, culturally relevant instruction, and data-informed practices. The staff also engaged in training on diversity, equity, and
inclusion (DEI) with a local expert throughout the year in addition to a team of staff that lead DEI work at the school. Annually
staff are provided opportunities for professional development in strategies for building a positive culture (Responsive
Classroom), supporting EL students (GLAD), and continued support and training in specific curricula (i.e. ERWC, CPM, Ready
Classroom, etc.).
Teachers in each academy regularly engage in professional learning communities (PLCs) to review assessment data, grades,
and other measures of academic learning to identify gaps and to develop strategies and programs to support student learning
and raise student achievement. Teachers new to the profession as well as teachers new to the school receive one-on-one
support from mentor teachers and peer coaching and support. As funding allows, NCS allocates $500 to each teacher
annually for professional development to be used at each person's discretion. These funds have been used by teachers and
administrators for attending workshops and conferences as well as for hiring substitutes to observe other teachers and to plan
in grade-level/department teams.
