General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.
Internet Access Internet access is available at public libraries and other locations that are publicly
accessible (e.g., the California State Library). Access to the Internet at libraries and
public locations is generally provided on a first-come, first-served basis. Other use
restrictions may include the hours of operation, the length of time that a workstation
may be used (depending on availability), the types of software programs available
on a workstation, and the ability to print documents.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
Sandburg Middle School is a place unlike any other—an innovative, twenty-first century school nestled against the awe-
inspiring Southern California foothills, a place where diversity is celebrated, collaboration flourishes, and students are
presented with opportunities for academic and personal growth on a daily basis. Sandburg has been honored by receiving both
the California Distinguished School Award and Gold Ribbon Award for our implementation of Advancement Via Individual
Determination (A.V.I.D.) strategies sitewide, benefitting all students on campus. At Sandburg Middle School, we believe all
students can meet high expectations on a daily basis. We focus on the academic and social emotional success of our students.
Our goal is to cultivate well-rounded, independent individuals who will become productive and fruitful members of our society.
There are many programs and intentional strategies that make Sandburg an exceptional school.
Unparalleled opportunities- Our talented faculty offers an expansive and challenging program in the arts, sciences, and
humanities. Our students are encouraged to be scientists, artists, athletes, mathematicians, musicians, designers, writers and
innovators. Our extracurricular activities, clubs, and sports programs are designed to complement student academic programs
while being engaging, relevant and fun. Unique clubs and activities such as our A.S.B., Honor Society and Interscholastic
Sports provide opportunities for students to excel outside of the classroom. Our clubs give students opportunities to connect
with like-minded friends and staff. All programs are designed to help build connectedness, confidence, and a school culture
that is inclusive of all students.
Each child is known and valued- Our diversity is our greatest strength. Students step on campus and are immediately greeted
by Dr. Porcell and Dr. Perez, our administrative team, as well as members of our incredibly friendly and caring staff. The
Sandburg staff and administration work extremely hard to understand the unique and diverse needs of each student. Success
is measured one child at a time, on an individual basis. Our academic program and qualified staff is supported by a variety of
tiered intervention supports that ensure all students have the support needed to access the curriculum and meet target goals.
Sandburg has two full-time counselors, a student support specialist, and a strong partnership with community agencies. These
supports allow Sandburg to offer intensive social emotional support to students and families through individual and group
counseling services. As Spartans, we believe in serving the whole child and differentiating programs to meet diverse student
needs.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
Sandburg was built in 1966 and has 36 classrooms, a cafeteria, an administrative office, a library, a gymnasium, locker rooms,
two computer labs, and a fitness lab. Outside facilities include large fenced in filed areas doe drills practice and organized

were sponsored by a community-voter supported Bond Measure G.

evaluation instrument developed by the Office of Public School Construction.

in high school (i.e., grade ten, eleven, or twelve).

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Sandburg is one of two middle schools in the Glendora Unified School District. We serve the western side of Glendora. Our
feeder elementary schools include La Fetra and Stanton, as well as a smaller percentage of students from Cullen, Sellers, and
Sutherland elementary schools. In addition to serving our Glendora residents residing within our school boundaries, Sandburg
is also home to students from surrounding districts attending our school on District of Choice, Allen Bill and interdistrict permits.
We are proud to accept students on transfers from within and outside of GUSD, pending space availability.
Sandburg is proud to partner with an active PTA which supports school programs and fundraises to provide additional
extracurricular events and activities for students and families. Some of the activities sponsored by PTA include school dances,
educational assemblies, volunteerism in classrooms and around campus, and parent education events. Staff work
collaboratively with PTA to bring enrichment opportunities to our students for increasing connectedness and a sense of
belonging. For additional information regarding parent involvement, please contact our PTA President Mrs. Teresa Quijano at
presidentsandburgpta@gmail.com.
We believe in involving all education partners in school decision-making and parents have many opportunities to volunteer on
campus and to assume leadership roles on campus. We encourage parents to get involved through PTA membership, as well
as serving as representatives on our School Site Council (SSC) and English Learner Advisory Committee (ELAC).
Participation in these school committees provides parents with opportunities to take on active roles in reviewing school
programs and protocols and to assist in guiding decision-making, with the goal of improving policies and practices.

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
School/district safety and disaster plans are evaluated annually and are available for review in the school office. This year, the
Comprehensive School Safety Plan was reviewed and approved by our School SIte Council on December 11, 2023. The
district has adopted a standard response protocol and standard reunification system and staff has been trained to respond
appropriately based on the emergency circumstance. Earthquake, disaster, lock-down, and fire drills are practiced regularly.
The PTA provides minimal food, water, and medical supplies for disaster preparedness. Sandburg utilizes Parent Square to
facilitate communication with students, staff, and families. In the event of an emergency, we are able to make school-wide
contact with parents and guardians. This year, we are piloting the Catapult Emergency Response System for quickly
communicating threats to safety on and around campus and to alert staff with timely information throughout any emergency or
crisis situation. Every year, the Sandburg staff works closely with our site Safety Team to refine emergency protocols and
update our comprehensive safety plan.--------

2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Fiscal Year 2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Sandburg School provides a Resource Specialist Program and a Special Day Class for students who qualify for Special
Education. These students receive specialized academic instruction (SAI) in smaller classroom settings with highly qualified
special education teachers and paraprofessionals. We also offer three ELA intervention sections, two ELD sections, and three
math intervention sections, providing small-group intensive support to identified students. Students are identified as requiring
additional intervention support through the Intervention Study Team (IST) process. Bilingual aides are assigned to support
English learners who are new to the country or who have been identified as performing at a Level 1 or 2 on the ELPAC
assessment.
Breakfast and lunch are offered to all students daily at no cost under the Universal Lunch Program. Additionally, identified
students may participate in the district Gifted and Talented Education (GATE) Program. We also house a parent-financed
Extended Day Care Program ion campus, before and after school.
Sandburg is proud to have been implementing the AVID Program for 17 years! AVID sections are offered to students in grades
6-8, and the majority of our instructional team has participated in AVID Foundational training, implementing WICOR strategies
across content areas. . Sandburg also offers advanced math classes in all grades as well as two Pre-Advanced Placement
English and U.S. History sections in 8th grade.
At Sandburg, we provide opportunities for intervention as well as enrichment. As such, teachers provide after-school math
tutorials and homework club opportunities for struggling students and serve as advisors for student-led social clubs that take
place during lunch periods and after school on a weekly or biweekly basis. After school math tutorials and homework club are
funded through Title I.

Professional Development
Professional Development
Staff training is provided throughout the school year in GUSD. The district calendar includes two orientation days prior to the
start of the school year in August, one staff development day in the fall, and one staff development day in the spring. Staff
development is ongoing and takes place throughout the year, by use of release time for teacher self-selected learning and
collaboration, district-provided or teacher/site initiated professional learning opportunities, and use of weekly early-out days.
Release time is continued weekly, using a collaboratively generated Professional Development to include district, site,
department, and teacher PD days. PD occurs each Thursday beginning at 1:30 p.m. For the 23-24 school year, Educator
Effectiveness Academies will also be implemented for individualized, self-selected professional development.
Site professional development will focus on site-based initiatives aligned with academic goals and district LCAP goals. Depth
and Complexity training will be one area of focus for improving instructional strategies and increasing critical thinking
opportunities in classrooms.
