General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.
Internet Access Internet access is available at public libraries and other locations that are publicly
accessible (e.g., the California State Library). Access to the Internet at libraries and
public locations is generally provided on a first-come, first-served basis. Other use
restrictions may include the hours of operation, the length of time that a workstation
may be used (depending on availability), the types of software programs available
on a workstation, and the ability to print documents.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
Mt. Olive Innovation and Technology High School provides educational programs and opportunities to meet the needs of all at-
promise students. We encourage students to take advantage of the programs we offer and for parents to take an active role in
their child’s education. The staff takes an active meaningful role in the life of all students in preparing them for college and/or
career. We believe all students are unique, have potential, can learn, and can succeed!
We prepare students for the 21st century global economy by insuring:
1) Integrated-Interdisciplinary and collaborative instruction is common-core based
2) Online Instruction is common-core based
3) Students are actively engaged in their learning, school and community
4) Assessment is used to ensure mastery
5) Students are college and/or career ready
6) Students are engaged in meaningful work-based learning activities
The MIT goal is to become “The model alternative high school in California” by providing high quality education to our learning
community that promotes personal responsibility, academic achievement, social emotional and professional growth, and
meaningful career preparation. Our educational environment fosters life-long learning and prepares our students for the 21st
century. Our mission is to provide a safe, supportive, and nurturing environment that encourages creativity, critical thinking,
collaboration and communication, while developing productive citizens who are college and/or career ready, and prepared for
life.
MIT serves a diverse ethnic and socioeconomic population within the community of Duarte and sections of an unincorporated
area of Los Angeles County. This is a community where some students come from homes with above average incomes and
others come from homes falling below the county’s poverty level. Approximately, 1% of our students are enrolled with intra-
district permits. Sixty-three percent (63%) of our high school students are male, 33% are female, 1% identify as non-gender.
MIT serves roughly 73 students in the ninth through twelfth grade and special education students through age 22. Due to the
nature of our unique student population, 81% of the students qualify for free and reduced lunch.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
MIT offers flexible, individualized educational programs leading to the attainment of a high school diploma and the fulfillment of
additional educational/career goals. Mt. Olive Innovation and Technology High School oversees five educational programs to
serve students: 9th Grade Opportunity; 10th -12th Continuation High School; Independent Study, Career Technical Education
and Special Education. These programs offer alternative approaches to traditional educational practices that fail to meet the
needs of our at-promise student population.
MIT is a WASC accredited high school and has received four consecutive Six-Year WASC Accreditation through the 2024
school year.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
MIT facilities are in good condition. There was the addition of one portable classroom added to the site in 2018 and there have
been no other significant additions or modernizations completed at MIT during the current year. MIT was built in 1961 and
comprises of seven classrooms, a main office, food service room, a counseling office, staff lounge, four restrooms, and an
outdoor basketball court. In 2018, MIT added an additional classroom to support its growing student needs. The site
administrator works closely with the MIT utility/custodian to ensure that cleaning of the school is maintained to provide a clean
and safe environment. The Duarte maintenance department ensures that the repairs necessary to keep the school in good
repair and working order are completed in a timely manner. Maintenance uses an online work-order process to ensure efficient
service and highest priority are given to emergency repairs.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
MIT has an active School Site Council (SSC) that encourages parent involvement. The voting membership are composed of
parents, students, community and staff. MIT School Site Council takes part in the development of School Plan for Student
Achievement (SPSA), the school safety plan, appropriate Title 1 spending, implementation and evaluation of instructional
programs. Parents are involved in the Student Study Team process and the English Language Advisory Committee (ELAC),
which runs through our SSC. Parents also attend parent education workshops through D'Veal Family and Youth Services, and
Parent University workshops held monthly. The annual Back-to-School Night and Open House events are held for students,
parents, and the community to provide information about innovative programs at MIT. Each year MIT participates in two
presentations to the Board of Education on school programs. One is on school programs, and the other presentation is the
School Plan for Student Achievement (SPSA).--------

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
The safety of staff and students is a primary concern and MIT complies with laws, rules, and regulations pertaining to
hazardous materials and state earthquake standards. The principal and School Safety Committee updated the latest School
Safety Plan on January 18, 2024, with the plan under yearly review and updated annually to be brought to the Board of
Education for approval in February 2024. We communicated all revisions of the plan to classified and certificated staff. The
Disaster Preparedness Plan includes steps for ensuring student and staff safety during a disaster. We schedule and conduct
fire, earthquake and lockdown drills on a regular basis throughout the school year. MIT certificated, classified and
administration supervises students before school, during break, during lunch and after-school. There is a designated area for
student drop-off and pick-up at the front gate. We direct all visitors to the main office where they are required to sign-in and
receive a visitor’s badge before walking on campus or entering classrooms.--------

2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Fiscal Year 2022-23 Types of Services Funded
MIT assist students with their social, emotional and personal development, as well as academics. The school gives special
attention to students who experience academic challenges, difficulty coping with personal and family dynamics, trouble with
decision-making or handling peer pressure. The following support programs are made available for all students at MIT: D’Veal
Youth and Family Counseling Mental Health Services, D’Veal Seeking Safety Class, D'Veal Girls and LGBTQ Empowerment
Groups; Acellus Social Emotional Learning (SEL) Online Class, Career & Health Interventions and Life Skills Literacy Speakers
(CHILL) Thursdays; Azusa Pacific Tutoring and After-School Tutoring; Peer-Counseling Groups for drugs and alcohol and
anger management; Academic and Career Counseling, English Language Development (ELD) , Special Education Support,
and Extended June School Year Program.
MIT strives to create an atmosphere that provides the tools to develop the “WHOLE CHILD” and transcends limitations. MIT
also provides career and aptitude assessment for students using YouScience, Armed Services Vocational Aptitude Battery
(ASVAB), Major-Clarity and Precision Exams for career certifications. MIT also offers a Planned Parenthood 6-week course, a
6-week City of Hope Youth Nutrition Education and Leadership Advocacy Class, an 8-weekYoung & Hungry Now Business
Entrepreneurship Program and a 3-week Financial Literacy program through Foothill Federal Credit Union. We are also a PBIS
School and utilize every opportunity to recognize and reward positive student outcomes through PBIS Rewards, our new digital
platform. We will continue developing of our Cinematic Arts Academy with Digital Immersive Scanning & Modeling; we are in
the second year of our cosmetology and barbering school partnership with Simply Divine; we are in year two of our Esports
Team; and in year 1 of our Music & Film class, Animation & Film class, History of Film, and development of our Film and
Finance math class for our cinematic arts pathway.
