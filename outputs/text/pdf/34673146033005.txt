By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to publish a School Accountability Report
Card (SARC). The SARC contains information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies (LEAs) are required to prepare a Local
Control Accountability Plan (LCAP), which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with
data reported in the SARC.
For more information about SARC requirements, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC Web
page at http://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/
For more information about the LCFF or LCAP, see the CDE LCFF Web page at http://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/
For additional information about the school, parents and community members should contact the school principal or
the district office.
DataQuest
DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest Web page at http://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that
contains additional information about this school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test data, enrollment, high school graduates,
dropouts, course enrollments, staffing, and data regarding English learners.
California School Dashboard
The California School Dashboard (Dashboard) https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new
accountability and continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and schools are meeting
the needs of California’s diverse student population. The Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of
LEAs, schools, and student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying strengths, challenges, and
areas in need of improvement.
Internet Access
Internet access is available at public libraries and other locations that are publicly accessible (e.g., the California State
Library). Access to the Internet at libraries and public locations is generally provided on a first-come, first-served basis.
Other use restrictions may include the hours of operation, the length of time that a workstation may be used (depending
on availability), the types of software programs available on a workstation, and the ability to print documents.
Admission Requirements for the University of California (UC)
Admission requirements for the UC follow guidelines set forth in the Master Plan, which requires that the top one-eighth of
the state’s high school graduates, as well as those transfer students who have successfully completed specified college
course work, be eligible for admission to the UC. These requirements are designed to ensure that all eligible students are
adequately prepared for University-level work. For general admissions requirements, please visit the UC Admissions
Information website at https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/.
Admission Requirements for the California State University (CSU)
Eligibility for admission to the CSU is determined by three factors: (1) Specific high school courses, (2) Grades in
specified courses and test scores, and (3) Graduation from high school. Some campuses have higher standards for
particular majors or students who live outside the local campus area. Because of the number of students who apply, a few
campuses have higher standards (supplementary admission criteria) for all applicants. Most CSU campuses have local
admission guarantee policies for students who graduate or transfer from high schools and colleges that are historically
served by a CSU campus in that region. For admission, application, and fee information, see the CSU website at
https://www2.calstate.edu.

School Description and Mission Statement (School Year 2024 - 2025)
At Charles Mack, our mission is to educate the whole child through equitable practices, exemplary instruction, and a
partnership with our community. For the 2023-2024 school year, Mack Elementary was again awarded the PBIS Gold
award for our efforts to incentivize students who follow our schoolwide expectations of being safe, respectful, and
responsible. Our staff, students and families have lived and embraced this mission and as a result, our students are
experiencing academic and social emotional growth. This year's school theme is: Today's Readers. Tomorrow's Leaders.
Our staff works hard every year to help students fill in academic gaps and bolster learning. We have made small group
instruction a priority as well as supporting our multi-lingual students and newcomers. In addition, there is a strong focus on
addressing the social emotional needs of students and teachers through classroom activities, an increase in wellness
providers, class discussions, the Second Step Program, reading, and individual projects. Teachers are making learning
meaningful and purposeful while attending to the thoughts, feelings, and personal expressions of their students. Because
of these efforts, we have vastly reduced the number of disciplinary occurrences as we focus on the whole child and
restorative practices.
Parent university events at Charles Mack teach modeled strategies that can be used by parents at home. In combination
with our expanding list of family-teacher run activities, the Charles Mack community continues to strengthen the existing
parent-teacher bond while promoting family and togetherness. In addition, our Champions after school program provides
all Charles Mack students with access to a menu of athletic, academic, and musical enrichment opportunities.
It is on these actions and commitments that we proudly engage our motto:
"From Mack, to Jackman, to Valley, to College"
Sincerely,
Samantha Moorer, Principal

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements (School Year 2024 - 2025)
School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements (School Year 2024 - 2025)
Charles Mack Elementary's main campus was originally built in 1965. Our school currently has 35 classrooms, a
multipurpose room, a library, a computer lab and an administration building. Additions were constructed in 1968 and 1972.
The current multipurpose room was built in 1990, and in 2000, additional permanent classrooms were added. In 2014, a
walkway, fencing, increased drainage and new sod were done, and an initial remodel for the parking lot has been
completed. In 2017, a new, extended parking lot replaced the old one. In 2021, cameras were added for campus
monitoring. In the summer of 2022, permanent buildings received new roofs and all classrooms were upgraded. In the
summer 2023, a new playground was created. New HVAC systems were recently installed in the office building.
The LEA takes great efforts to ensure that all schools are clean, safe, and functional. To assist in this effort, the LEA uses
a facility survey instrument developed by the State of California Office of Public School Construction. The results of this
survey are available at the district office. Included is information from the facility survey instrument on the condition of the
school and the efforts made to ensure that students are provided with a clean, safe, and functional learning environment.
Our school received an "Exemplary" status based on the State's definitions for each of the eight categories evaluated,
which is the highest rating possible.

The district’s Maintenance and Grounds departments work in concert with the school site custodial team to ensure that
school buildings, classrooms, and grounds are maintained at exceptional levels. The district utilizes the latest electronic
work order system enabling site administration and custodians to communicate maintenance needs, urgent repairs, or
necessary projects and emergency repair needs are immediately resolved. School’s custodians work as a team and with
the administrators to develop a daily cleaning process and schedule. Each morning the custodian inspects the school prior
to students and staff entering school grounds. Restrooms are inspected throughout the day for safety and sanitation. The
Board of Education has adopted cleaning standards for all schools in the district.
The State Deferred Maintenance Program (DMP) was established in 1980 to aid districts in major maintenance projects.
Beginning with the 2013-2014 fiscal year and the inception of the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) the State has
intended for Deferred Maintenance needs and expenses to be included in the District's LCFF allotment. The district
continues to fund routine projects previously considered as part of the State's former DMP at levels required by the State
and continues to use local resources.
Thanks to the district’s governing board, the Facilities and Planning department, and the support of the Charles Mack
community, modernization projects have been done inside and outside our buildings. The HVAC system in the A wing was
replaced in the of summer 2024. Both the Kinder and main playgrounds were refreshed all in the summer and fall of 2024.

School Safety Plan (School Year 2024 - 2025)
The School Site Council reviewed and approved the Comprehensive School Safety Plan on September 27, 2024, to
include documentation of new safety precautions including a new Kinder gate and an office buzzer door system, and
improved suspension data. From 7:30 when breakfast starts to 5:30 when our Champions after school program ends,
students who are on campus are safe from harm and well supervised. Access to the school is controlled through locked
gates at entry points around the school, and visitors are required to sign in at the office upon arrival and get either a visitor
sticker or lanyard to wear while they are on campus. We are a closed campus from 8:10am to dismissal. Our Emergency
Response Procedures are available in every classroom, and students have numerous opportunities to practice them
through various preparatory and practice drills throughout the year.

Types of Services Funded (Fiscal Year 2023 - 2024)
Mack provides numerous programs and supports for students. Our workshop intervention model provides daily small group
instruction student based on identified needs. Support from highly qualified AITs (Academic Intervention Teachers),

room on campus, school staff and district student mentoring, and boosting attendance. Lastly, Champions provides
athletic and academic enrichment and music, as pillars of our after school program.

Professional Development
Charles Mack teachers have either been trained or will be trained in how to use Guided Language Acquisition and Design
(GLAD), Guided Reading, and close reading strategies to help Mack students develop skills and access information
across contents. This year, our teachers have increased their use of social-emotional learning activities through the
school-wide implementation of the Second Step Program. Our district has adopted Illuminate to provide common
assessments which are used to inform instruction and guide lesson planning. Each staff member is also required to
complete courses with the EPOCH Education program including Understanding the Power of Language Acquisition and
Communicating and Partnering with All Families. The teaching staff has also been provided training for our new math
program: Envision as well as sessions for effective small group instruction. Our district continues to provide professional
development in a variety of areas of need and concern to our staff. At Mack, staff professional development is relevant and
timely planned in an effort to meet the changing needs of our staff.
