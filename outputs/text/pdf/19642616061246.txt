General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.
Internet Access Internet access is available at public libraries and other locations that are publicly
accessible (e.g., the California State Library). Access to the Internet at libraries and
public locations is generally provided on a first-come, first-served basis. Other use
restrictions may include the hours of operation, the length of time that a workstation
may be used (depending on availability), the types of software programs available
on a workstation, and the ability to print documents.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
This School Accountability Report Card is designed to provide you with a general understanding of Foothills Middle School and
Arcadia Unified School District. Keep in mind that this report card describes information for the 2022-2023 school year.
Middle school years are a time of transition. A supportive, nurturing, and positive environment is critical in a successful middle
school. As you review our School Accountability Report Card, you will see that our core beliefs are evident. These beliefs serve
as the foundation for everything we do at Foothills Middle School. We believe that everyone should be treated with dignity and
respect. We believe that a partnership between parents and the school is essential in meeting the ever-changing needs of our
young people. We believe everyone benefits from an environment that promotes student learning, accountability, collaboration,
wellness, and innovation. We believe that all students need to be “connected” to our school. We believe that it is our
responsibility to set high standards for achievement and to support our students in meeting and exceeding those standards.
Foothills Middle School is dedicated to academic excellence and to the personal, physical, and social development of students.
All students are challenged to reach their full individual potential in a positive, student-centered, teacher-guided environment.
Our staff believes that a school-wide emphasis on providing opportunities for students to develop sound decision-making skills
will help prepare them in their transition from the elementary school level to high school and beyond. In recognition of our
school’s focus on excellence, Foothills was honored in 2022 as a National Blue Ribbon School, and in 2013 with the California
Distinguished School award.
In the Arcadia Unified School District, families and the community are partners for student success. The policies, personnel,
and resources of the District ensure that all students are provided engaging and meaningful learning opportunities. As a result,
students are challenged to think and reason, to develop responsibility for lifelong learning, to respect diversity, and to make a
positive and profound impact on their world. It is the mission of the District and the obligation of each school to provide quality
work that engages students, has meaningful content, and challenges every student to learn more.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
Foothills Middle School was built in 1961 and provides a safe, clean environment for students and staff. All schools in the
Arcadia Unified School District underwent complete renovation from 1995–1996.
Foothills Middle School campus currently has 35 classrooms, one staff room, a library/media center, a multipurpose room, two
locker rooms, and two athletic fields. At the time of this publication, all of the school’s restrooms were fully functioning. The front
of Foothills Middle School was re-landscaped in 2002. In 2003–2004 the patio in the quad area was enlarged and trees and
benches were added. In 2005–2006 the planters by the main building were re-landscaped. In 2006–2007 a curved brick wall
was constructed along the driveway entrance to the school. In 2007–2008 a brick planter was constructed in the quad and was
landscaped with flowers, ferns, and a tree.
Measure I was passed by the community in 2006. This general obligation bond resulted in major upgrades to all classrooms
and facilities. Construction began at Foothills in April 2013 and was completed during the 2014-2015 school year.
In 2018, artificial turf was installed near the library, providing a beautiful and low-maintenance landscape, and new sheet vinyl
flooring was installed in the game room.
In 2019, the Restorative Circle Garden project was completed, providing classrooms and student groups with a place to hold
community circles and class meetings outdoors in a tranquil setting. Facilities projects including a student wellness center, a
student leadership room, a digital communications studio, and an office quiet room--which support both the academic and
social-emotional needs of students--are currently in progress.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Parents and the community are highly supportive of the educational, cultural, and enrichment programs at Foothills Middle
School. Parents are a vital component in Foothills Middle School’s school-to-home partnership. The school’s dedicated Parent
Teacher Student Association (PTSA) supports numerous activities throughout the school year, including theme days, grade-
level field trips, the annual book fair, classroom projects, Red Ribbon Week, Open House, the National PTA Reflections
program, and Back-to-School Night.
The Parent Music Club supports the student band and orchestra programs at Foothills by providing volunteers for special
events and funding the school’s participation in music festivals and parades. Parents also serve as active members of the
School Site Council (SSC) and English Language Advisory Committee (ELAC). The Chinese Parent Club meets monthly to
help newly-arrived families access school resources and partner with school personnel for student success.
Parents who wish to participate in Foothills Middle School’s leadership teams, school committees, school activities, or who
wish to volunteer, can contact the school at (626) 821-8363 or visit the school’s website at https://fh.ausd.net.

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
To safeguard the well-being of students and staff, a Comprehensive School Safety Plan has been developed by the Safety and
Disaster Committee which consists of classified staff, teachers, and the principal. The committee sets goals for the school to
continually improve in three areas: School Climate (students, parents, and programs), Emergency Preparedness (disasters and
crisis), and School Environment (facilities and campus). The plan addresses various safety issues, including creating
procedures that detect and prevent bullying, ensuring appropriate campus supervision, maintaining disaster kits, and
monitoring emergency procedures. The Comprehensive School Safety Plan is updated each year and approved by the School
Site Council and the Board of Education. The plan is reviewed with all staff members at the beginning of each school year.
Safety drills are held on a regular schedule: fire drills are held five times per year, earthquake drills are held four times a year,
and lockdown/shelter-in-place drills are held at least three times per year. Staff members also receive disaster training on a
regular basis. Many staff complete CPR and First Aid training.
To ensure student safety, teachers, administrators, and school proctors supervise students on campus before and after school
and during break and lunch. Foothills Middle School is a closed campus, and permission is always required for students to
leave school grounds. All visitors to the campus are required to check in at the school’s main office and must wear the proper
identification badge at all times.

Professional Development
Professional Development
Arcadia schools offer staff members professional development that is both district and site-coordinated. Staff development
topics include effective pedagogy, assessments, using data to inform instruction, developing critical thinking, implementation of
the electronic grade book, the California Academic Standards, identification, development, and curation of online resources,
pedagogical shifts, brain research, Universal Design for Learning (UDL) and Innovative Instruction. Focus has shifted to
developing protocols to fully implement Multi-Tiered Systems of Support, including equitable practices in education to support
the needs of every learner.
Individual school sites have been tasked with working alongside the Instructional Coaches Team to support professional
development around the California Academic Standards, including rigor, focus, coherence, instructional strategies, shifts in
curriculum, pacing and instructional materials, writing instruction, and problem-solving.
Multiple data points are reviewed and evaluated to design and determine professional development needs. Survey data is
collected to identify needs from all stakeholders, bargaining unit forums offer both qualitative and quantitative data to determine
staff needs. Self-reflection tools are used to determine professional development needs for standards implementation.
Professional development sessions are determined by the various needs determined by stakeholders and state mandates.
Professional development has traditionally been delivered by in-district training and local or regional conferences. Virtual
professional development offerings developed during COVID-related school closures via virtual synchronous and
asynchronous training have remained in use as an additional option for staff, both internally and from outside organizations.
This extends professional development opportunities beyond traditional in-person instruction, including during staff,
department, grade-level meetings, and staff development days.
Arcadia’s innovative, collaborative structures allow for teachers to meet together on a regular basis: The Small Working Arcadia
Groups (SWAG) meet seven times a year and in the past focused on teacher-selected topics such as Universal Design for
Learning (UDL), Multi-Tiered Systems and Support (MTSS), or NGSS (Next Generation Science Standards), Opportunities for
teachers have continued to enhance the work of community-building in the classroom, the social and emotional needs of
students, and supporting positive student behavior.
