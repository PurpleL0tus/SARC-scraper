General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
For over a hundred years, Escondido Union High School District (EUHSD) has been a source of inspiration and support for
students, helping them reach their goals. Whether in classrooms, libraries, or sports fields, students at Escondido, Orange
Glen, San Pasqual, Valley, and Del Lago Academy have diverse opportunities to learn and excel. With over 7,400 students,
EUHSD is dedicated to public education.
Escondido High School (EHS), established in 1894, is the oldest school in EUHSD. Located in Escondido, a vibrant city with a
rich history, EHS collaborates with parents, the community, and local businesses to prepare students for success. With nearly
1,900 students, EHS offers a nurturing environment for grades 9-12, emphasizing academic success, community involvement,
and a variety of interests.
EHS, the second oldest high school in San Diego County, has a modern campus with upgraded facilities, including a Learning
Center and auxiliary gymnasium. The school embraces a broad range of programs, from a flourishing farm and agriculture
program to cutting-edge labs in woodshop, graphic design, culinary arts, and more. The Visual and Performing Arts (VAPA)
programs cover photography, dance, choir, and various art forms.
The Advanced Placement program at EHS offers over 21 courses for a challenging academic experience. The school boasts a
vibrant extracurricular environment with 30 clubs, after-school programs, and academic support initiatives. Inclusivity is a
priority, encouraging students to participate in 30 different sports at novice, Junior Varsity, and Varsity levels. Unique offerings
include the Dual Language Immersion Program and Dual Enrollment courses with Palomar Community College, allowing
students to earn college credits alongside graduation credits.

2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
Escondido Union High School District sets a high priority upon ensuring that sufficient and current textbooks and materials are
available to support instructional programs. Escondido Union High School District held a Public Hearing on September 12,
2023 and determined that each school within the district has sufficient and good quality textbooks, instructional materials, or
science lab equipment, pursuant to the settlement of Williams vs. the State of California.
All students, including English Learners, are given their own individual standards-aligned textbooks and/or instructional
materials in core subjects and visual and performing arts, for use in the classroom and to take home. Textbooks and
supplementary materials are adopted according to a review cycle established by the district. Materials considered for adoption
are reviewed by committees of teachers, administrators, and parents, and a recommendation is made to the School Board by
the Instructional Program Review Committee. All recommended materials are available for parent examination at the district
office prior to adoption. The schools also utilize a variety of novels and other instructional materials throughout their curriculum.

2022-23 Career Technical Education Programs
Career Technical Education Programs
In alignment with California Department of Education, Escondido Union High School District defines career paths as a program
of study that involves a multiyear sequence of courses that integrates core academic knowledge with technical and
occupational knowledge to provide students with a pathway to postsecondary education and careers. The CTE department
collects data on the number of students moving through our local sequences. Some CTE courses, because of the time
requirements, internship element and industry standards they meet, may be defined as a sequence within themselves.
CTE courses are found throughout our schools. A CTE concentrator is identified locally and is a student who has completed 50
percent of a planned program sequence (in hours or credits) in a state-recognized CTE sequence and is enrolled in the next
course in that sequence or has completed 50 percent of a single state-recognized multi-hour course and is enrolled in the
second half of that course. Capstone courses are defined as completer based on length of course, sequence, and skill
development. The CTE capstone course leads to a certificate/industry skill sheet or license if applicable. The program also
provides the student with academic and technical knowledge and skill through integrated academic and technical instruction.

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
The Escondido Union High School District prioritizes the safety and security of our students and staff through a comprehensive
approach to emergency preparedness. We collaborate with the Escondido Police Department, employing dedicated school
resource officers and six full-time campus supervision assistants. Additionally, our administrators and staff actively participate
in overseeing school grounds and ensuring a safe environment during all school events.
To maintain a secure campus, we enforce a closed campus policy and require all visitors to register with the office, ensuring
that our school community remains protected. We conduct a thorough annual review of our school safety plan, collaborating
closely with the Escondido Police and Fire Departments. Staff members receive regular training, equipped with disaster plan
books and trained in their effective implementation.
We prioritize proactive emergency preparedness by conducting regular drills, including evacuation procedures and fire drills.
These practices involve coordination with local and regional emergency response agencies, ensuring that our students and
staff are well-prepared for any situation.
In the event of a serious emergency, parents can find reassurance in the fact that our school buildings are earthquake safe,
equipped with fire-fighting equipment, and have detailed plans and procedures for various emergencies. Our dedicated staff
prioritize the safety and welfare of our students. During emergencies, students are expected to follow the directions of teachers,
staff, or bus drivers to ensure their safety.
We appreciate the cooperation of parents in vital areas such as refraining from calling the school, keeping streets clear for
emergency vehicles by avoiding driving to school, and tuning in to local radio stations KKLQ 600 or KCBQ 1170 on the AM dial
for important information and directions. In case of emergencies, the family reunion area is established at Wilson Stadium on
the EHS campus, ensuring a centralized and secure location for reunification.

2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Fiscal Year 2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Escondido High School (EHS) offers a diverse range of courses and programs to enhance students' educational experiences.
This includes Honors classes in English, optional Honors credit in Math courses, and a comprehensive selection of Advanced
Placement classes across various departments. The school provides online classes for both advancement and remediation, as
well as a full-time online Independent Study program through the on-campus Learning Center.
The school employs various intervention providers to support student needs, including a school social worker, a school
psychologist, a bilingual family liaison, and six school counselors. Collaborating with community resources, EHS facilitates
access to intervention teams such as the Family Intervention Team (FIT), the Prevention and Diversion (PAD) program, and
Safety Outreach for Students (SOS) program.
EHS features Grade 9 and 10 Academies, involving six Teachers on Special Assignment (TOSAs) dedicated to coordinating
supports and interventions for students in these grades. The counseling department at EHS operates as a comprehensive
school counseling program, aligned with the ASCA National Model. The counseling team assists students with academic,
career, and personal/social needs through various delivery methods.
English Learners at EHS receive core instruction in all content areas, including English Language Development (ELD).
Specially designed academic instruction in English, bilingual instructional aides, and small class sizes support English Learners
in their academic progress. Special Education services are tailored to individual student needs and may include interpreters,
trained aides, special day class placement, case managers, collaborative teacher placement, and access to a sensory room.
The school offers Advancement via Individual Determination (AVID) courses during the school day to prepare students for
college eligibility and success, supported by trained tutors. The Student Support Team (SST) process is utilized to identify,
monitor, and provide interventions for students at risk, involving collaboration among various stakeholders.
EHS benefits from additional funded positions to support students, including a College and Career Technician, Library Media
Technician, a Student Health Office staffed by a full-time and part-time Student Health Care Specialist, and an Athletic Director
to coordinate on-campus sports teams. As a Title 1 school, EHS receives supplemental federal funding to further support its
students. The comprehensive approach to education at EHS underscores its commitment to meeting the diverse needs of all
students.

Professional Development
Professional Development
Escondido High School (EHS) and the Escondido Union High School District (EUHSD) are dedicated to continuous
professional development for their staff. This includes various training methods such as site workshops, staff meetings, district-
led sessions, conference participation, and individual mentoring. The professional development programs are crafted based on
input from teachers, student needs, and current requirements. Implementation is monitored through various forums, including
PLC teams, department meetings, and surveys.
In collaboration with EUHSD's Educational Services department, EHS supports staff through ongoing professional
development using the Arc of Learning framework. This involves working closely with administrators, teacher leaders, and
instructional coaches to construct studio cycles in areas like Social Emotional Learning (SEL), Improvement Science, Design
Thinking/Equity Protocol, and Deeper Learning.
EUHSD has prioritized professional learning and instructional material training by hiring Content Specialists in critical academic
areas, directing resources toward professional learning in various domains, and regularly engaging staff through surveys and
listening sessions.
Professional Learning Communities (PLCs) are integral to EHS, where teachers collaborate weekly in content-specific teams to
address essential questions related to student learning. These include expectations, assessment methods, interventions, and
enrichment strategies. The focus is on a short-term cycle of inquiry to address instructional challenges.
Restorative Practices and Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS) are emphasized in professional development
sessions. Teachers learn about creating positive classroom cultures, setting expectations, implementing restorative responses,
and fostering communication between school and family.
For academic rigor, EHS staff collaborates with the San Diego County Office of Education (SDCOE) to develop rigorous tasks
and assess question levels in assignments. Administrators and Content Specialists ensure alignment with the Hess Cognitive
Rigor Matrix.
Job-specific professional development is provided, with counselors, special education, security, and administrative staff
receiving training relevant to their roles. Mathematics and science teachers undergo specific training, and English Language
Arts teachers are equipped with the latest materials and methodologies.
