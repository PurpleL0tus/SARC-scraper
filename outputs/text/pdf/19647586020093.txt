General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.
Internet Access Internet access is available at public libraries and other locations that are publicly
accessible (e.g., the California State Library). Access to the Internet at libraries and
public locations is generally provided on a first-come, first-served basis. Other use
restrictions may include the hours of operation, the length of time that a workstation
may be used (depending on availability), the types of software programs available
on a workstation, and the ability to print documents.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
Principal's Message
The Los Nietos STEAM Academy staff is pleased to have been recognized as one of the top public schools in Los Angeles
County for English Learners in English Language Arts multiple years by Innovate Public Schools and the University of Southern
California. As educational leaders we at Los Nietos Middle School STEAM Academy act as role models for our students; We
believe in demonstrating a love for learning; We believe in providing a safe and nurturing environment where every student will
be treated as an individual who deserves excellence in education. We believe in providing middle school students with
opportunities and experiences that can shape them as learners and citizens. As a staff, we are committed to the core values of
education and a strong foundation that all students can succeed. We provide rigorous learning opportunities so that students
are able to meet or exceed state standards in all content areas. A collaborative effort, active learning, diversity, and respect are
valued. High expectations for academic achievement and social development are top priorities in developing our school’s
culture of success as we help students to discover their pathway towards College and Careers.
LNMS STEAM offers a wide array of course offerings to meet students’ diverse needs and to motivate them to excel. Our
STEAM (Science Technology, Engineering, Art, & Mathematics) program includes advanced mathematics courses; Project
Lead the Way and MESA (Mathematics, Engineering and Science Achievement) elective courses as well as coding, beginning
and intermediate band; visual arts; flight/ aviation; and digital yearbook design. Other electives at LNMS include ELASupport,
Math support, and Health and Fitness. In addition, our school offers after school enrichment opportunities like CyberPatriot
competitions, Girls Who Code, ESports, Student Council, and Students Run LA Marathon program. We participate annually in
an intramural sports program in the areas of volleyball, flag football, soccer, and basketball. After-school supervision,
enrichment, clubs, and physical activity are provided by Boys & Girls Club of Whittier.
Los Nietos Middle School STEAM Academy Vision Statement
Los Nietos Middle School believes in developing the whole child. We provide a rigorous curriculum that assists ALL students in
mastering state content standards. We maintain high expectations and respect for students, staff, families and community
members.
Los Nietos Middle School Mission Statement
At Los Nietos Middle School, we value the rights and responsibilities of citizenship, collaborative effort, and active learning. We
strive to show that we value diversity, respect cultural and linguistic differences, and believe that all students are imminently
educable. We hold both staff and students accountable for their work.
We provide curriculum and instruction that meets or exceeds state standards. As a school-wide Title I school, we seek to meet
the needs of all students, including special education students, general education students, English language learners, Gifted
and Talented Education students, and those students who are at-risk. Our class offerings rotate each quarter as the needs of
our students change. We believe in purposeful staff development opportunities that empower teachers, classified staff and
administration to be life-long learners practicing to perfect our craft. As our assessment and standards drive our curriculum,
teachers use this information to differentiate and modify instruction in order for our students to reach proficiency.

Textbooks
Textbooks are based on the California Content Standards and are selected by committee in a pilot process from lists that have
been approved by the State Board of Education. For a list of some of the textbooks we use at our school, see the Data
Almanac that accompanies this report. We have also reported additional facts about our textbooks called for by the Williams
legislation of 2004. LNMS STEAM passed it's last Instructional Materials Sufficiency visit with no insufficiencies on 9/6/23.
During the 23-24 school year we will finalize the adoption of our new History Social Science materials.
Computers
Computer skills and concepts at Los Nietos Middle School STEAM Academy are integrated throughout the standard curriculum
and aligned to the District Technology Use Plan, which prepares students for technological growth and opportunities. Each
classroom teacher has a laptop and ipad for instructional use in language arts, mathematics, social studies, and science. All
LNMS STEAM students have access to a chromebook in each content area class as well as a device checked out to them for
home use. In addition, LNMS STEAM has a computer labs that teachers may visit with classes. For additional use in the
classroom/library setting, a laptop cart, and two carts of tablets are available to support learning across content areas. Students
have the opportunity to create a variety of projects on the computer related to what is being taught in the classroom. Software
includes programs to develop critical thinking, digital media, word processing, and presentation skills.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
The district Director of Maintenance and Principal of LNMS STEAM inspects the school campus twice a year for safety
hazards. If any hazards or repairs are discovered, they are corrected immediately. They inspect classrooms, the cafeteria, staff
lounge, outdoor areas, our gym, auditorium, library, and restrooms. The District meets monthly with all site custodians to
discusses safety issues and provides training to help mitigate safety concerns. LNMS has passed it's Williams Facilities
Inspection for the year on 8/28/23.
Our school buildings are 75 to 80 years old, our classrooms underwent a full interior modernization including wiring, electrical,
wifi upgrades, tackable wall surface, flooring, and cabinetry completed in the 2017-18 school year. All buildings were brought
up to ADA compliance in the modernization. Our HVAC system was completely replaced over the 2012 - 14 school years. The
restrooms are cleaned and stocked on a daily basis. We have one day custodian and one night custodian. Our custodians work
closely with our district's maintenance staff to keep the campus free of litter and graffiti, and regularly check the grounds and
equipment for safety hazards. Landscaping upkeep is provided by the district's maintenance team at all sites.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Opportunities for students’ families to participate in the school are provided throughout the year. LNMS holds School Site
Council (SSC), English Language Advisory Committee (ELAC), parent meetings with the Principal, ASB school-wide activity
events, and Parent Education classes. Parent education topics are offered in many areas such as: transitioning to middle
school, bullying prevention, attendance monitoring, health, and nutrition and transitioning to High School. Rio Hondo College
offers both Basic Computers and English as a Second Language classes to parents of Los Nietos Middle School free of charge
through their continuing education department. Back-to-School Night, as well as fall and spring parent-teacher conferences are
well-attended and enable parents to demonstrate their support of students and our school's overall program. Our families also
are offered opportunities at the district level to participate in various district wide parent workshops, which are designed to
empower parents as partners in education.--------

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
We review and update our Comprehensive School Safety Plan annually. The school plan is updated each year and is shared
with staff and parents. It includes disaster procedures, district and site policies, a discipline plan, and other safety information.
Large storage containers on campus house disaster supplies that include food, water, and emergency materials. Fire,
earthquake, and lock-down drills are executed monthly. The school participates annually in the Great Shake Out. In 21-22 a
significant portion was added to the plan and regarding student safety as they return to school from Distance Learning and
continues COVID protocols.
Staff members including; campus supervisors, counselors, and administrators monitor the school grounds before and after
school, during the nutrition break, and at lunchtime. Our campus gates are secured during the day, and visitors must enter the
school through the locked main office to sign in. Once on campus, guests receive a visitor’s badge to wear throughout their
stay for identification.

2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Fiscal Year 2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Our intervention program is divided into two areas: during-school hours and after-school hours. Academic Support and English
Language Development classes are assigned as electives for students needing additional academic support in foundational
language, literacy, and mathematics skills during their regular school day. After school intervention includes ST Math, short
term standards based ELA intervention, math reteaching and retesting on specific concepts and homework assistance.
Students feel supported and are able to increase their assessment scores and proficiency levels through their participation in
various interventions. In addition to Intervention programs LNMS STEAM offers enrichment opportunities for student after
school. Included in these enrichment are Coding, CyberPatriots, ESports, Sports, offered by school site staff and additional
programing offered by the Boys & Girls Club of Whttier.
Many resources are available on-site that supports students' social development and well-being. A full-time counselor, in
conjunction with a team of guidance interns, offers academic advisement to students. Pacific Clinics, as well as Counseling
interns, provide small group counseling sessions weekly on campus to select students. A therapist from Pacific Clinics provides
individual and family counseling to students and their families who qualify. All students at LNMS STEAM also have access to
Hazel Health Tele-Therapy for free.
Both state and federal dollars are spent to support student learning, effective instruction, and standards-based curriculum
implementation. General funds are used to provide instructional materials, support for professional development, and
assessment programs. Federal Title I funds support additional programs and resources such as a community outreach
facilitator, academic counseling, tutoring intervention for students, and instructional materials to support achievement. Title II
funds support ongoing professional development and collaboration in Professional Learning Communities. Title III funds
support intervention for English learners outside of the school day, supplemental instructional materials, parent education
opportunities, and high-quality professional development and collaboration opportunities for staff.

Professional Development
Professional Development
High levels of collaboration and articulation are key components of our staff professional development plan. Teachers at the
middle school meet during their prep period twice a week and on Tuesdays after-school for professional development and/or
grade level planning. Middle school teachers attend training alongside teachers from the District’s elementary schools as part
of our District professional development offerings. To monitor the instructional program, the principal meets with grade level
teams at the end of each common unit assessment to reflect on data. Also, throughout the year, principals and district
administrators provide feedback and reinforcement to aid academic action planning. LNMS teachers attend articulation
meetings with teachers from the Whittier Union High School District to align practices and review student performance data.
