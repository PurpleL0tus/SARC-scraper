School Description and Mission Statement (School Year 2023–24)
Pathways to College K8 is a classroom-based charter school currently serving 380
students in grades TK-8. 80% of students at PTC are residents of Hesperia and would
have otherwise attended schools within Hesperia Unified School District. The remaining
20% of students reside in the surrounding communities of the Victor Valley area. PTC
will continue to serve students TK-8 in the high desert area. PTC will accept all students
who are residents of California who meet enrollment and attendance/participation
requirements for proximate, distance, and/or hybrid learning.
PTC’s student population reflects the racial, ethnic, cultural and economic diversity of
HUSD and surrounding communities. As such, PTC will continue to attract, enroll and
educate students who are classified as low-achieving, economically disadvantaged, at-
promise, and those who may require a smaller setting. These are the students of the
high desert area.
The need for a school that serves this population specifically is demonstrated by District
and Charter school’s demographic data. All schools that PTC students would otherwise
have attended present with similar statistics: high levels of low-performing students,
homeless/foster youth, and socio-economically disadvantaged. Both District schools
and PTC are striving to become schools in which these at-promise students can achieve
success.
MISSION
The mission of Pathways to College K8, is to provide all students with a safe and
challenging learning environment that emphasizes college preparation. PTC will create a
culture that promotes academic growth, social-emotional maturity, and positive
behaviors.
VISION
We envision a school that stands apart from others in its ability to influence all who enter
its gates in a positive way; a school wherein each student is able to become a life-long
learner and productive member of society; a school whose purpose is to develop in
every learner a regard for the potential that each of us has.
KEY PROGRAM ELEMENTS
Culture of High Expectations
Pathways to College K8 embraces a culture of high expectations. High Expectations are
both the conscious and unconscious anticipations of positive growth held by each
stakeholder group at PTC for themselves and for each of the other groups. Of all the
possible expectations that any stakeholder group could have for themselves and others
at a school, the most important are those that focus on the student as held by the
teacher (Hattie, 2017).
Furthermore, because growth in the areas of social emotional learning (SEL), behavior,
and academics is interdependent, teachers and staff must have high expectations in
each. For example, a student who is emotionally immature will most likely not be able to
manage his/her behavior effectively in a classroom setting, will not see the value in
obtaining an education, and therefore will not achieve his/her academic potential.
Similarly, a student who is not achieving his/her academic growth potential will likely
have feelings of self- doubt and discouragement, which can lead to behavior problems.
Developing a growth mindset is key to developing a culture of high expectations. As one
develops a growth mindset, one is able to acknowledge more fully that growth is
possible for everyone else.
This growth mindset is based on the belief that your basic qualities are things you can
cultivate through your efforts. Although people may differ in every which way in their
initial talents and aptitudes, interests, or temperaments, everyone can change and grow
through application and experience. (Dweck, 2007)
A culture of high expectations is created when stakeholders have positive expectations
for their own and others’ growth. This is developed and expressed in a number of
different ways. What follows are just ten of the many ways in which PTC will develop and
promote expectations of student growth.
1. Helping Students to View Challenges as Opportunities
2. Acknowledging and embracing imperfections
3. Differentiating and Valuing Processes for Growth
4. Learning about the Brain’s ability to Be Flexible
5. Using the word “Learning” instead of “Failing”
6. Developing a Purpose/Motivation for Growth
7. Celebrating Hard Work that Leads to Growth
8. Avoiding Descriptors Such as “Good Boy” or “Smart Girl” 9. Using the word “Yet” instead of
“Can’t”
10. Making New Goals When Others Are Accomplished
Methods of communicating these high expectations to students are detailed in the
section How Learning Best Occurs. Without high expectations, student learning is
minimal.
School Goals
The school has four goals which are derived from the mission and vision statements.
These four goals as as follows:
1. PTC will promote academic growth of students.
2. PTC will promote positive behaviors of students.
3. PTC will promote social emotional learning of students.
4. PTC will create a safe, warm and welcoming environment.
Progress Indicators
As a charter school, PTC uses standardized tests approved by the California Department
of Education for purposes of charter renewal every 2-5 years. The assessments
selected by PTC include the following:
1. California Assessment of Student Performance and Progress (CAASPP)
2. English Language Proficiency Assessments for California (ELPAC)
3. Northwest Evaluation Association's Measures of Academic Progress (NWEA MAP)
4. Amplify's mClass
Metrics for each of these assessments are outlined in the charter petition itself. The
overarching goal for each of these assessments is that a preponderance of students is
showing growth each year that the assessments are given. These metrics are reported
to the board each year in a regularly scheduled board meeting in the fall or winter of the
following school year as the data become available. Progress monitored in this way is
also reported to the District every two to five years during the renewal process.
Schoolwide Programs
Pathways to College K8 offers a variety of programs that help to achieve the above
mentioned four goals. These are described below:
S.M.A.R.T. Club - Pathways to College makes use of its Title I funds to support the
hiring of Intervention Aides who operate a pull-out program designed to meet the
academic needs of students in ELA and math. Students who need the extra support
are taught in small groups throughout the day over six-week periods at which time the
groups are reorganized, and then the pull-outs begin again. SMART stands for Skills
Mastery and Academic Readiness Training.
After School Tutoring - Pathways to College uses grant monies from the Expanded
Learning Opportunities Program to operate an after school tutoring program for
students who struggle to meet target growth. Groups consist of 2-4 students maximum
and run for 30 minutes after school is over.
Saturday Make and Take Workshops & Field Trips - Using grant monies from the
Expanded Learning Opportunities Program, PTC is able to offer all students on a first-
come-first-serve basis a Saturday workshop and field trip opportunity on as many as 20
Saturdays throughout the year.
Summer School - Pathways to College offers summer school to students who are
prioritized for attendance based on academic need. The program runs for three weeks in
the month of June.
SPECIALS - Specials are taught to all students in grades TK-5 by two fully credentialed
teachers for up to 35 minutes per day. The subjects taught are P.E. and STEAM,
and students participate in one or the other alternately each day of the week.
ELECTIVES - Engaging elective classes are taught to students in grades 6-8. Anywhere
from 6-7 elective classes are offered each semester from which students may choose.
High Desert Athletic League - HDAL, as it is known, is an after school sports program
for students in grades 6-8. Students practice various sports and compete with other
schools in the high desert. Sports that Pathways to College has competed in include
cross-country, basketball, and soccer.
After School Education and Safety Program - ASES, as it is known, is offered to
approximately 100 students and operates from 3:15 to 6:00 p.m. daily. The program
offers homework help, physical and enrichment activities, and a supper to all enrolled
students. The program is free.
The National School Lunch Program - Pathways to College is able to offer all students
a free breakfast and lunch each day of the school year.
Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS) - Pathways to College takes a
positive approach to managing students behaviors. Students are taught correct
behaviors, given the opportunity to practice them, and are incentives with tokens called
CatCash that they can use at the PTC student store once month.
California Integrated Supports Project (CA-ISP) - The CA-ISP is a program
sponsored by the California Department of Education to improve application of culturally
responsive teaching, restorative justice, and trauma-informed instruction. Pathways to
College was selected by the Desert Mountain SELPA to pilot the program in the high
desert.
NEW BUILD PROJECT - As Pathways has come to serve an increasing number of
students each year, it has become necessary to find another location that better meets
the needs of students and staff. Pathways has embarked on an ambitious new build
project in Hesperia that will eventually serve upwards of 700 students. The new build is
located just three minutes from the current location and ground breaking has already
started. They completion date is sometime in the fall of 2024.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
Pathways to College K8 currently leases space from its authorizing District, Hesperia Unified School District.
Maintenance of the facilities is a joint project. Pathways to College K8 (PTC) has an operations manager who oversees
the facility needs of our campus. Repairs and maintenance concerns are either taken care of by PTC staff members or
referred to the District who performs major repairs in a timely fashion. PTC's custodial staff members maintain our
building and provide scheduled routine maintenance. We take great efforts to ensure that our school is clean, safe, and
functional. Food and restroom facilities are cleaned and maintained throughout the day to ensure the health and safety of
students and staff.
General
The LEA takes great efforts to ensure that all schools are clean, safe, and functional. To assist in this
effort, the LEA uses a facility survey instrument (Facilities Inspection Tool or FIT) developed by the
State of California OPSC. The results of this survey are available on the Internet at
www.pathwaysk8.com, at the front office, or by request to the principal by emailing
craig.merrill@pathwaysk8.com.?
Maintenance and Repair
PTC maintenance staff ensures that the repairs necessary to keep the school in good repair and
working order are completed in a timely manner. A work order process is used to ensure efficient
service and that emergency repairs are given the highest priority. Major repairs are conveyed to the
District from which the property is leased through a work order process as well.
New School Construction Project
Pathways to College is currently engaged in a new build project on a 12 acre site just three minutes
from the current location. The site will eventually house upwards of 700 students in grades TK-8. The
completion date for the project is currently scheduled for October of 2024.

B. Pupil Outcomes
State Priority: Pupil Achievement
The SARC provides the following information relevant to the State priority: Pupil Achievement (Priority 4):
Statewide assessments (i.e., California Assessment of Student Performance and Progress [CAASPP]
System includes the Smarter Balanced Summative Assessments for students in the general education
population and the California Alternate Assessments [CAA] for English language arts/literacy [ELA] and
mathematics given in grades three through eight and grade eleven. Only eligible students may participate
in the administration of the CAAs. CAA items are aligned with alternate achievement standards, which are
linked with the Common Core State Standards [CCSS] for students with the most significant cognitive
disabilities).
The CAASPP System encompasses the following assessments and student participation requirements:
1. Smarter Balanced Summative Assessments and CAA for ELA in grades three through eight and
grade eleven.
2. Smarter Balanced Summative Assessments and CAA for mathematics in grades three through
eight and grade eleven.
3. California Science Test (CAST) and CAA for Science in grades five, eight, and once in high school
(i.e., grade ten, eleven, or twelve).
College and Career Ready: The percentage of students who have successfully completed courses that
satisfy the requirements for entrance to the University of California and the California State University, or
career technical education sequences or programs of study.

School Site Council
The School Site Council is a representative body comprised of elected or volunteer members who
represent parents, students, faculty, staff and the local community. School Site Council
composition, functioning and responsibilities will follow regulations established by the California
Department of Education.The School Site Council will work with school leadership to discuss,
develop, review and evaluate school improvement programs and school budgets. The members of
the Council are elected by their peers. For example, parents elect the parent representatives and
teachers elect teachers.
Over the course of a year, a typical Council objective might consider certain goals of the school
and then work with school leadership to evaluate the school's progress toward those goals. In this
evaluation, the council might consider school test scores, attendance and discipline records,
parent surveys and input from students. The Council may also create plans for improvement in
certain academic or operational areas of the school. Plans might involve a new academic program,
staff member or parent outreach strategy. For example, a Council objective might be to help raise
or re-direct funds to develop a new math program, while another objective might work toward
hiring a reading specialist. Yet another Council objective might work toward hiring an additional
teacher to reduce class sizes in a particular grade or a parent liaison to get more parents involved
at the school.
ELAC/DELAC Committee
Pathways to College shall comply with the provisions of Education Code Section 52176(b) for the
establishment and operation of an English Language Advisory Committee (ELAC). If Pathways to
College meets the criteria set forth in Education Code Section 52176(a) for the establishment of a
District English Language Advisory Committee (DELAC), it shall also establish a DELAC. The
English Learner Advisory Committee is composed of the principal, two teachers, and four or more
parents whose children are English learners at PTC. The committee is a subcommittee of the PAC
and has a separate meeting time and agenda. Members of the ELAC may also be members of the
PAC. The ELAC advises the school administration and provides reports to the PTC board on the
following:
1. Development of a school plan for education programs and services for English learners.
2. Conducting a school-wide needs assessment.
3. Establishment of school goals, and objectives for programs and services for English learners.
4. Development of a plan to ensure compliance with any applicable teacher and/or teacher aide
requirements.
5. Review and comment on the school district reclassification procedures.
6. Review and comment on the written notifications required to be sent to parents and guardians.
7. Review and comment on the development or annual update of the Local Control and
Accountability Plan (LCAP).

Suicide Prevention Policy
Pursuant to the requirements of Ed. Code §215, the Charter School's governing board
shall ensure that its policy on pupil suicide prevention in grades 7 to 12, inclusive, was

CHILD ABUSE REPORTING AND PROCEDURES
All persons who are mandated reporters are required, by law, to report all known or
suspected cases of child abuse or neglect. It is not the job of the mandated reporter to
determine whether the allegations are valid. If child abuse or neglect is reasonably
suspected or if a pupil shares information with a mandated reporter leading him/her to
believe abuse or neglect has taken place, the report must be made.
DISASTER PROCEDURES
DUCK, COVER, AND HOLD ON is the action taken during an earthquake to protect
students and staff from flying and falling debris. It is appropriate action for earthquakes
and explosions. Evacuation is implemented when conditions make it unsafe to remain in
the building. The evacuation plan includes procedures on how to assist individuals with
disabilities. Lockdown is initiated to protect students and school staff from danger when
there is a threat on campus and evacuation is not an option. Shelter in place is a short-
term measure implemented when there is a need to isolate students and staff from the
outdoor environment to prevent exposure to airborne contaminants. Convert School is
implemented during a disaster when a requirement exists for community medical facilities
or shelters. If the Red Cross chooses a school as a community shelter, it will arrange
shelter set-up.
SUSPENSION AND EXPULSION POLICY
All offenses will be handled in a confidential manner on an individual basis. When
disciplinary action is referred to the Executive Director/Principal or designee, the
student's parent/guardian will be notified by telephone. A disciplinary report is written that
requires the signature of the student, parent/guardian and involved staff. At the discretion
of the Executive Director/Principal or designee, a meeting will be held with the student,
the parents and involved staff members to discuss ways to improve the student's
behavior. However, parents/guardians may at any time request a conference with the
Executive Director/Principal or designee to discuss their son/daughter's behavior.
Disciplinary action may include suspension for minor acts and expulsion from the school
for major offenses. Suspension is intended to remove the student
