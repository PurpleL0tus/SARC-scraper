General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
Ada S. Nelson Elementary School is one of four schools in the Los Nietos School District located in an unincorporated area of
Whittier within Los Angeles County. Our school has 379 students in grades Transitional Kindergarten through 6th grade. The
demographics of the pupil population is 96% Hispanic, and 4% other. Approximately 26% percent of the students are English
learners. The students have a variety of life experiences. Some pupils have recently arrived from outside of the U.S. while
others have lived in the Los Nietos neighborhood for their entire lives. All Nelson students qualify for Title I and are eligible for

Nelson students are served by dedicated individuals, including a principal, teachers, and support staff. Parents indicate that

Vision Statement:
Nelson Elementary School is dedicated to becoming a community of students, parents, and staff who are committed to lifelong
learning in a clean, safe, and healthy environment. The school community is devoted to maintaining high academic and
behavioral standards while focusing on the social-emotional well-being of all students.
Mission Statement:
The mission of Ada S. Nelson Elementary School is to provide a safe, caring environment where students can best develop the
skills and character necessary to successfully navigate their communities and our global society.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
Our school buildings are 55 to 65 years old, but some of them were modernized in the early 1990s and again in 2019. We have
portable classrooms ranging from two to 12 years old. The restrooms are cleaned and stocked daily. We have a day and a
night custodian and they work closely with our maintenance staff to keep the campuses clean from litter and graffiti, as well as
regularly check the playground equipment for safety hazards. Landscaping upkeep is provided by the district's maintenance
team at all of our campuses.
More facts about the condition of our school buildings are available in an online supplement to this report called for by the
Williams legislation of 2004. What you will find is an assessment of more than a dozen aspects of our buildings: their structural
integrity, electrical systems, heating and ventilation systems, and more. The important purpose of this assessment is to
determine if our buildings and grounds are safe and in good repair. If anything needs to be repaired, this assessment identifies
it and targets a date by which we commit to make those repairs. The guidelines for this assessment were written by the Office
of Public School Construction (OPSC) and were brought about by the Williams legislation. You can look at the six-page
Facilities Inspection Tool used for the assessment on the Web site of the OPSC.
Library
We have a spacious, inviting library that holds approximately 7,000 volumes and has four student computer stations. Our
library technician staffs the library five days a week, and parent volunteers check out books and read to children on a regular
basis. The library stays open until 3 p.m., so that students can check out books after school. Every year we budget
approximately $5,000 for new books, audio/visual equipment, and other resources for our collection from the proceeds of our
annual book fair and other funds provided by the state.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Throughout this year, our Parent Teacher Council (PTC) has worked to provide multiple opportunities for parent involvement
and volunteering. Our Parents have been able to assist with planning school activities, assisting teachers with preparing class
materials, making copies, and providing other means to support academic instruction. We train our parent volunteers so that
they will meet our standard of conduct and work in concert with the staff to provide a safe learning environment for our
students.
Our Scholastic Book Fair, Halloween Trunk or Treat, Winter Wonderland schoolwide choral performance, Read Across
America Day, and monthly fundraising events provide opportunities to increase parent and family engagement. Our PTC has
been very successful in working with our community to support student learning and to continuously build and maintain positive
relationships with our families.
Our School Site Council (SSC) and our English Learner Advisory Council (ELAC) provide input and assist with the decision-
making, monitoring of school programs, and implementing programs that support all students. Additionally, our "Coffee with the
Principal" meetings have become a viable means of reciprocal communication for parents and school administration. We are
consistently working to build stronger connections with parents and community members as we support our students in all
academic endeavors.

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
Nelson annually reviews the School Safety Plan, and the plan was last reviewed and revised in January 2023. The school plan
includes disaster procedures, policies, discipline plans, and other safety information. The School Safety Plan is shared with the
staff, and we practice fire, earthquake,evacuation, and lockdown drills regularly. A comprehensive safety drill called the Great
Shakeout is executed each year, where all staff and students simulate an earthquake, evacuation, search and rescue, as well
as parent pick-up/reunification procedures. These drills actively keep staff and students alert to possible incidents and
prepared with the proper protocols and procedures to follow in the event of an actual emergency.
Our earthquake storage containers include food, water, and emergency supplies that are inspected annually with contents
being replaced as needed. The district's safety consultant inspects the school campus twice a year to look for safety hazards
in the classrooms, the cafeteria, the staff lounge, the media center, the playgrounds, and the restrooms. Any hazards that are
discovered are corrected immediately. The district safety committee meets multiple times each year to discuss safety issues,
training, and other safety-related concerns. In addition, staff members monitor the school grounds before and after school,
during recess, and at lunchtime. Our campus is closed, and visitors must enter the school through the administration office
where identification is verified. Once a visitor has signed in, they receive a badge that must be worn and remain visible
throughout their visit.

Professional Development
Professional Development
Districtwide professional development is focused on literacy through the Science of Reading strategies and providing
integrated/designated English Language Development to our language learners. As a school site we continue to focus on the
GRR lesson design while implementing a tiered intervention model and assessing students during instructional time using
frequent checks for understanding. We provide students with small group instruction both in class and through an intensive
structured pull-out intervention program designed to support mastery of grade-level standards and to build foundational skills.

Professional Development
Teachers received professional development in structuring these classes, correlating the ELD and ELA standards, and using
appropriate resources to integrate learning across content areas. Additionally, all teachers have received professional
development to support our students in writing including Handwriting Without Tears. All grade levels participate in data-driven
discussions that provide teachers time to collaborate and plan next steps for instruction. We have also placed an emphasis on
Social Emotional Learning (SEL) and have been trained on CHAMPS and Second Step curriculum to address the mental
health concerns and overall well-being of all students.
