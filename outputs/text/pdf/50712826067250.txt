General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.

Program Descriptions
School Based Coordinated Program (SBCP)
Title I, Title II, Title III, and Special Education programs are included in the School-Based Coordinated Program (SBCP). These
programs provide all students, including English Learners (EL), Gifted and Talented (GATE), and Special Education students
with a rich variety of supplemental materials, equipment, activities and/or personnel. The School Plan describes Title I, Title II,
and Title III, and methods for each program's evaluation. The School Site Council (SSC) oversees the development and
implementation of the school site plan.
Academic Adviser
A full-time academic adviser is employed to help students with academic needs, conflict resolution and emotional support.

Student Success Team (SST)
The Student Success Team convenes upon receiving referrals from either the administration or the academic adviser. The
purpose of these meetings is to deliberate on the unique requirements of students referred by staff or parents. Comprising a
site administrator, teachers, and the school academic advisor, the team endeavors to pinpoint potential obstacles hindering the
student's success. Subsequently, they offer suggestions to parents and teachers, aiming to address the specific needs of the
student. In certain cases, the team may propose gathering more information about the student before making
recommendations or suggest referring the student for Special Education or 504 eligibility.
English Learners Program (EL)
The philosophy of the English Learner (EL) program is rooted in providing an intensive English-Language Development
Program for all English learners, aiming to enable their academic and social development to the fullest extent. Aligned with the
EL Roadmap Policy adopted by the California State Board of Education on July 12, 2017, the program is built upon four key
principles:
1. **Assets-Oriented and Needs-Responsive Schools:**
Pre-schools and schools demonstrate responsiveness to diverse EL strengths, needs, and identities. They foster socio-
emotional health and development, valuing and building upon the cultural and linguistic assets that students bring to their
education. The programs emphasize safe and affirming school climates, and educators actively engage in building strong
family, community, and school partnerships.
2. **Intellectual Quality of Instruction and Meaningful Access:**
English learners are immersed in intellectually rich, developmentally appropriate learning experiences promoting high levels of
English proficiency. These experiences integrate language development, literacy, and content learning. Meaningful access is
facilitated through native language instruction and scaffolding. ELs have access to a standards-based and relevant curriculum
to develop proficiency in English and other languages.
3. **System Conditions that Support Effectiveness:**
Every level of the school system is equipped with leaders and educators knowledgeable about and responsive to the strengths
and needs of English learners. Valid assessments and data systems inform instruction and continuous improvement.
Resources and tiered support are provided to ensure strong programs and build the capacity of teachers and staff to meet the
needs of English learners.
4. **Alignment and Articulation Within and Across Systems:**
English learners experience a coherent, articulated, and aligned set of practices and pathways across educational segments.
This continuity begins with a strong foundation in early childhood and extends through reclassification, graduation, and higher
education. The pathways foster skills, language(s), literacy, and knowledge necessary for college- and career-readiness in a
global, diverse, multilingual 21st-century world.
**EL Program Goals:**
1. Comprehend spoken English.
2. Communicate ideas orally in English.
3. Read and comprehend in English for success within the regular program.
4. Write in English using correct grammar and vocabulary.
5. Share cultural background through classroom/school activities to enhance self-worth.
All English learners not reclassified enroll in English Language Development (ELD) classes alongside benchmark ELA classes.
Reclassified students' progress in the core program is regularly monitored, and long-term English learners (LTELs) receive
coaching and support for re-designation. Teachers hold Cross-cultural Language and Academic Development (CLAD),
SB1969, or SB395 certificates. Bilingual paraprofessionals assist in providing ELD instruction. Services undergo review by the
School Site Council, the principal, and the English Learner Parents Advisory Committee (ELPAC).
English learners receive designated ELD from appropriately trained teachers, with integrated ELD instruction occurring
regularly in content area classes. Cooperation between the classroom teacher and EL staff is crucial to ensure appropriate
instruction. Trained EL paraprofessionals support teachers and students across core subjects: ELA, Social Studies, Math, and
Science.

Honors Courses
Prescott provides Honors classes in ELA, Math, Science, and Social Studies to cater to the requirements of High Achieving and
GATE students. Alongside these honors courses, we offer Pre Algebra in 7th grade and Algebra in 8th grade. Students who
successfully complete Algebra at Prescott are exempt from taking it in high school.
Elective Courses
Prescott Junior High School offers a wide array of elective courses that include Educational Technology, PLTW, Yearbook,
Conversational Spanish, Spanish 1, e-journalism, Leadership, Drama, Band, Orchestra, and Art.
Every year, Prescott students participate in Academic Pentathlon, Science Olympiad, Spelling Bee, and National History Day
competitions organized by the Stanislaus County Office of Education. Our students have won top prizes in all of these
competitions.
California Junior Scholastic Federation (CJSF)
Our CJSF students participate in various community service activities and they organize and attend educational field trips.
Special Education
Special Education students at Prescott receive instructional services tailored to their Individualized Education Plan (IEP).
Collaboration among specialists, staff, and classroom teachers ensures the provision of an optimal program in the least
restrictive environment. In the 2023-2024 academic year, Prescott has introduced a co-teaching model within the resource
program, where resource program students are integrated into regular classrooms based on their IEPs. They are also enrolled
in resource classes as outlined in their IEP. Testing services are administered by the school psychologist, speech therapist,
and resource specialist.
The District extends psychological, speech, and adapted physical education services. Two Resource Specialists (RS) and two
instructional paraprofessionals cater to students with specific needs. Additionally, the District operates two Learning
Handicapped Special Day Classes (SDC) at Prescott, staffed with two SDC teachers and two instructional paraprofessionals.
Oversight and coordination of all site special education services fall under the purview of the school principal and vice-principal.
Beyond specialized instruction, Special Day Class students seamlessly integrate into the regular program, assigned to
appropriate classes as specified in their IEPs. Enrichment opportunities abound for SDC students during the advisory period,
including participation in elective courses, field trips, and other enrichment activities.
Special Education is included in the School-Based Coordinated Program.
Non-identified Special Needs students may be selected to receive supplemental assistance from special education staff
through teacher referrals to a student study team or a classroom teacher, special education teacher and site administration.
Priority will be given to those non-identified students with the greatest risk of not meeting grade level standards in language arts
and/or mathematics. Supplemental assistance may be in a pull-out or classroom setting and include one-on-one or small group
instruction.
Centralized Services
Centralized services, consisting of the Assistant Superintendent for Curriculum and Instruction, the Assistant Superintendent
for Student Services, one Program Specialist, and two Administrative Assistants, offer the following support: advising school
site personnel on state and federal program matters, providing expertise on legal information, and, when required, meeting the
requirements for the development and/or revision of district applications and written school plans. These services also extend
to assisting site administrators, staff, and parents with program review coordination and processes, encompassing compliance,
internal evaluations, and quality assessments.
Students who are identified as in the low socio-economic subgroup will receive primarily indirect services.

internal connectivity and ensures wireless Internet access in every classroom. This technological infrastructure has enabled the

After School Athletics -
Prescott boasts a robust After School Athletics program offering various opportunities for student participation, including girls'
volleyball, boys' and girls' flag football, boys' and girls' basketball, boys' and girls' track, and boys' and girls' wrestling. The
school adheres to a no-cut policy, ensuring that all interested and eligible students have the chance to participate. Emphasizing
character development and sportsmanship, these values are integral themes within the after-school athletic program. Funding
sources for the program may include contributions from the Parents' Club and the District. Additional fundraising activities or
donations are often required to fully implement and support the program.
Homework Policy -
Prescott School has a homework policy in place. Homework is viewed as a necessary part of students' learning. A copy of the
policy is given to parents, and it is also in student planners. Homework must also be connected to the curriculum and is to be
graded if assigned.
Parent Involvement Policy -
Parents are strongly encouraged to actively engage in their child's educational journey through various avenues. They play a
vital role in supporting fundraising initiatives for special student activities, such as field trips. Parental involvement is facilitated
through organizations like the Parents' Club, Parent Advisory Committees, School Site Council, and English Learners' Parents
Advisory Committee, offering parents a direct voice in planning and implementing the school program.
Numerous avenues, such as newsletters, Back-to-School Night, Parent-Teacher Conferences, and Open House, provide
additional opportunities for parents to participate, fostering their connection with the school and gaining insights into school
programs and their child's educational progress. To ensure effective communication, interpreters are available for all parent
meetings, and notices are disseminated in both Spanish and English. Written forms of the Grade Level Standards are provided
and explained to all interested parents during regularly scheduled conferences.
Teacher Recruitment
It is the policy of Stanislaus Union School District to seek out, hire and support highly qualified teachers in every classroom.
Recruitment efforts include online job postings, recruitment fairs throughout the area and extensive newspaper ads. All other
provisions of Public Law 107-110 are in compliance.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
The district consistently conducts regular inspections of site facilities. Annually, the Board undertakes a campus tour to verify
that all facilities adhere to the district's standards of excellence. Additionally, local fire officials conduct routine inspections to
ensure the facility's safety and compliance with State standards for fire safety. Recent inspections have yielded positive results.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Prescott Junior High School extends a warm welcome and is dedicated to fostering parent involvement in the educational
journeys of their children. Numerous avenues are available for parents to engage, including Back-to-School Night, parent
conferences (fall/spring), School Site Council, English Learner Advisory Committee, Parents' Club, Student Store, District

has proven to be a valuable tool in involving parents in their children's education. Additionally, teachers and administrators
maintain regular communication with parents via email and phone, addressing various issues concerning their children and
seeking parental input on crucial decisions related to their children's education.

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
Prescott Junior High School has embraced a Safety Plan that undergoes an annual review and revision process led by a
dedicated Safety Committee and the School Site Council. These committees convene a minimum of four times a year to
assess whether any immediate addendums are required for the safety plan. The Safety Committee, consisting of the principal,
vice principal, teacher representatives, classified employees, local law enforcement, parents, and students, collaborates to
review and approve the latest plan. The comprehensive plan comprises sixteen sections, covering various aspects such as
emergency response actions, incident response team coordination, shelter in place procedures, and more.
The finalized safety plan undergoes scrutiny and approval from key entities, including the School Site Council, Site Leadership
Team, PBIS Team, and the SUSD School Board. Additionally, several members of Prescott Junior High School have actively
participated in Active Shooter Trainings at various elementary schools and the junior high school, collaborating with Modesto
City Police. This drill has proven beneficial in preparing the district and school for potential emergencies. Subsequent to the
Active Shooter Training, all staff engaged in discussions during a faculty meeting to enhance campus safety and emergency
preparedness. Safety proposals and operations will be regular agenda items during Prescott Monday faculty meetings,
ensuring that all faculty and staff are well-versed in procedures and prepared for any emergency scenarios.

2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Fiscal Year 2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Supplemental services aimed at aiding limited English proficiency students are partially financed through Economic Impact Aid
(EIA) funds. A School Violence and Safety grant has contributed to covering the expenses of a campus supervisor. Title I, II, III,
Lottery, and LCAP funds are utilized for direct instructional support for students.
For the 2020-21 academic year, the allocation of funds is as follows:
• **Title I:** Funding for professional development, collaboration days, consultants, instructional supplies, student
planners, and digital technology.
• **Title I Parent Involvement:** Funds dedicated to supporting parent activities and involvement.
• **Title II:** Allocation for the professional development of teachers, with a focus on Response to Intervention training,
utilizing data for effective instruction, and aligning with Common Core State Standards through curriculum, common
formative assessments, Professional Learning Communities, and EL instruction strategies.
• **Title III:** Funds designated for professional development to assist teachers in effectively serving the educational
needs of EL students.
• **TUPE:** Utilization of funds for various activities promoting anti-tobacco awareness among students.
• **Site:** Allocated funds to support student academic achievement and school operations, covering expenses such
as promotion ceremony materials, outside services for the ceremony, postage, postage machine lease, academic
achievement awards, PBIS awards, instructional materials, office materials, toner, and copy machine agreements.
• **LCAP:** Funds used for providing materials and supplies for academic intervention, library and PE materials,
teacher training in digital devices.
• **Lottery:** Funding for instructional materials, supplementary instructional materials, student academic achievement
awards, and office materials and supplies.

Professional Development
Professional Development
Prescott teachers actively engage in district-wide professional development, encompassing Response to Intervention (RTI) and
effective instruction. Some educators have further enriched their knowledge by attending Solution Tree's Response to
Intervention and Professional Learning Conferences. These conferences have proven instrumental in providing tools and
support aligned with our overarching goal of ensuring every student achieves high levels of academic success.
In the aftermath of Professional Learning Community (PLC) trainings, our teachers persistently refine their learning outcomes
and success criteria. To facilitate this ongoing process, we have instituted weekly 60-minute PLC sessions every Wednesday,
coinciding with early student release. During these collaborative sessions, our teachers focus on several key areas, including:
• Engaging in Professional Learning Communities
• Implementing Response to Intervention strategies
• Developing and designing targeted interventions
• Curriculum design and calibration
• Data analysis, particularly in terms of student achievement
• Professional collaboration time to formulate and analyze SMART Goals
• Designing and developing Common Formative Assessments
Additionally, beyond districtwide initiatives, the site actively participates in monthly training sessions facilitated by a consultant.
She collaborates with each content area to select essential standards, build units centered around these standards, create
common formative and summative assessments, and utilize data for both intervention and enrichment purposes.
