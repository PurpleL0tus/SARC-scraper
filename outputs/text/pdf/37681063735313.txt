General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
Orange Glen High School, one of three comprehensive high schools in the Escondido Union High School District, is situated in
the eastern part of Escondido, California, about 40 miles northeast of San Diego. Escondido's economy encompasses retail,
services, light manufacturing, and agriculture. Orange Glen High School, established in 1962 on 48.5 acres, boasts modern
facilities, including classrooms, a media center, a performing arts center, two gymnasiums, a football stadium, sports fields, and
an administrative wing. The campus features a well-equipped weight room, upgraded drama facility, and specialized spaces
like an online Learning Center, auto mechanics shop, commercial kitchen, ceramics art center, print shop, and computer
graphics lab. The school is known for its beautifully landscaped and well-maintained environment, resembling a park.
Vision:
Orange Glen High School envisions every student being empowered to:
• Embrace Learning
• Achieve their Personal Best
• Build their Social, Emotional, and Physical well-being
Mission:
The mission of Orange Glen High School is to empower the school community by establishing safe spaces that foster
confidence, collaboration, and risk-taking. The aim is to create a vibrant community of educators, parents, and future leaders.

2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
2023-24 Quality, Currency, Availability of Textbooks and Other Instructional Materials
The Escondido Union High School District places a strong emphasis on ensuring that there are ample and up-to-date textbooks
and materials to support instructional programs. Following a Public Hearing on September 12, 2023, the district confirmed that
each school within its jurisdiction possesses satisfactory and high-quality textbooks, instructional materials, and science lab
equipment as mandated by the settlement of Williams vs. the State of California.
Every student, including English Learners, receives their individual standards-aligned textbooks and/or instructional materials
for core subjects, as well as visual and performing arts. These resources are intended for both classroom use and for students
to take home. The district adopts textbooks and supplementary materials based on a review cycle, with committees consisting
of teachers, administrators, and parents evaluating the materials. The Instructional Program Review Committee then makes
recommendations to the School Board. Before adoption, all proposed materials are made available for parents to examine at
the district office. Additionally, schools incorporate various novels and instructional materials into their curriculum.

2022-23 Career Technical Education Programs
Career Technical Education Programs
In alignment with California Department of Education, Escondido Union High School District defines career paths as a program
of study that involves a multiyear sequence of courses that integrates core academic knowledge with technical and
occupational knowledge to provide students with a pathway to postsecondary education and careers. The CTE department
collects data on the number of students moving through our local sequences. Some CTE courses, because of the time
requirements, internship element and industry standards they meet, may be defined as a sequence within themselves.
CTE courses are found throughout our schools. A CTE concentrator is identified locally and is a student who has completed 50
percent of a planned program sequence (in hours or credits) in a state-recognized CTE sequence and is enrolled in the next
course in that sequence or has completed 50 percent of a single state-recognized multi-hour course and is enrolled in the
second half of that course. Capstone courses are defined as completer based on length of course, sequence, and skill
development. The CTE capstone course leads to a certificate/industry skill sheet or license if applicable. The program also
provides the student with academic and technical knowledge and skill through integrated academic and technical instruction.
The programs prepare students to enter current or emerging careers for further training within the career path for which there is
gainful employment. All students are provided practical experiences to pursue post high school employment and gain course
work that is A-G compliant.
OGHS offers the following CTE pathways and courses:
Digital Photo-Advanced Digital Imaging
Visual Communications
Advanced Visual Communications
Video, Advanced Video
Multimedia Communication
Advanced Multimedia Communication
Culinary Arts & Food Science-Advanced Culinary Arts & Restaurant Management
Culinary Arts & Food Science-International Cuisine
Culinary Arts & Food Science-Advanced Baking
Print and Graphic-Page Layout and Design
Print and Graphic-Screen Print
Auto Technology 1-Auto Technology 2
Students who engage in the pathways find relevance in their studies, are provided an opportunity to apply their academic and
industry skills to everyday problems, are taught to think critically, and are required to act professionally. Through the IEP
process, special education students may be scheduled into CTE courses. Students are provided the support and necessary
curriculum modification as outlined in the IEP. CTE provides extended testing time, extra assignment time and support where
appropriate. Since our CTE courses work to integrate and support the academic standards, the students will be exposed to
“hands on” activities and participate in solving real life problems. The capacity to serve a “significant” number of students and
the degree to which the program attains specific outcomes are assessed yearly. Completion rates, graduation rates, and
completion of a sequence are all evaluated yearly. The number of completion certificates issued, and the attainment of specific
industry skills is analyzed. Data on attendance is also collected.

2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
2023-24 Opportunities for Parental Involvement
Orange Glen High School takes pride in fostering strong connections with parents through various school and community-
based organizations. The school's Parent Education Program and English Language Advisory Committee (ELAC) organize
multiple parent education series each semester, typically totaling 16 sessions annually, drawing attendance from 10 to 100
parents. This program offers classes, specialized assistance, and a platform for parents to engage with school staff.
Several booster organizations, including music boosters and athletic boosters, actively contribute support to school programs.
The family liaison plays a crucial role in connecting parents to school resources, coordinating weekly events for both English
and Spanish-speaking families, and providing interpretation during School Site Council meetings to facilitate the participation of
Spanish-speaking parents in their native language. The OGHS Family Engagement Center serves as a collaborative space for
parents to work together, access technology for checking students' grades and attendance, and attend workshops.
In addition, the school's social workers are available to assist parents in finding local resources such as healthcare, mental
health care, affordable housing, and legal assistance that might be challenging to access independently. This commitment to
family and community involvement is a vital element of Orange Glen High School's intervention and student support strategies.

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
The district prioritizes school safety by employing school resource officers from the Escondido Police Department and six full-
time campus supervision assistants. Administrators and staff actively contribute to supervising school grounds, providing
security, and overseeing all school events. The campus is maintained as a closed environment, and all visitors are required to
register with the office. The school collaborates with local police and fire departments to review and modify its safety plan,
conducting staff training regularly. The safety plan undergoes an annual review.
Orange Glen High School's Safety plan, last reviewed in November 2022, includes an emergency plan addressing various
situations. Staff members are equipped with disaster plan books and receive training in its implementation. The entire school
practices evacuation procedures annually with local and regional emergency response agencies. Fire drills are also conducted
throughout the year.
Parents are advised that, in serious emergencies, students may be safer at school than at home due to earthquake-safe
buildings, on-site firefighting equipment, and comprehensive emergency procedures. The safety and welfare of students are
prioritized during emergencies. Students are expected to follow the directions of teachers, staff, or bus drivers if en route.
Parents are requested not to telephone the school during emergencies to keep lines available for responders, avoid driving to
school to allow open streets for emergency vehicles, and tune in to designated radio stations for information. The family
reunion area is designated at the Orange Glen High School Stadium. The school encourages families to create home
emergency plans in alignment with the Escondido Union High School District Disaster Preparedness Plan.

2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Fiscal Year 2022-23 Types of Services Funded
Orange Glen High School provides a diverse range of courses and programs to enhance students' learning experiences. These
include Honors and Advanced Placement classes, as well as online courses through the Learning Center. Since 2019, the
school has offered dual enrollment courses in collaboration with Palomar College, allowing juniors and seniors to take college
classes during the school day, including options like Chicano Studies.
In August 2020, Orange Glen introduced a new program called Middle College at Orange Glen, a school within a school for
dual enrollment. This unique opportunity allows students to take at least one Palomar College class each semester starting in
9th grade. Enrollment is voluntary, and the application process considers students' academic skills, past performance, and
potential. Counselors play a pivotal role, meeting with students annually to review academic progress and post-secondary
goals and providing seniors with guidance on post-secondary plans.
To support academic success, Orange Glen High School has implemented a robust intervention system. In the last two years,
House Leads were added to provide targeted interventions for ninth graders. These supports encompass academic
intervention and referral to social workers, family liaisons, and additional school counselors. The intervention team includes the
Family Intervention Team (FIT), the Prevention and Diversion (PAD) program, and Safety Outreach for Students (SOS)
program.
The Orange Glen High School Counseling Department follows a comprehensive school counseling program aligned with the
ASCA National Model. Counselors act as advocates, leaders, collaborators, and system change agents for student success.
They address academic, career, and personal/social needs through classroom sessions, small groups, and individual settings.
The counseling team actively works to enhance academic success through academic enrichment and intervention programs.

Professional Development
Professional Development
Orange Glen High School (OGHS) and the Escondido Union High School District (EUHSD) are deeply committed to the
continuous professional development of their staff. Various training opportunities, including site workshops, staff meeting
activities, district-led initiatives, conference attendance, and individual mentoring, are provided. These offerings are
meticulously designed based on student and staff needs, incorporating teacher feedback and addressing current educational
requirements. The implementation of professional development is actively supported and monitored through Professional
Learning Community (PLC) teams, department meetings, PLC lead teacher meetings, all-staff meetings, and the review of
surveys and student data in small groups.
In collaboration with EUHSD's Educational Services department, OGHS actively supports staff through the ongoing
professional development framework known as the Arc of Learning. This involves close collaboration with site administrators,
teacher leaders, and instructional coaches to co-construct studio cycles. These cycles specifically focus on areas such as
Social Emotional Learning (SEL), Improvement Science within PLCs, Design Thinking/Equity Protocol, and Deeper Learning
via Studio Cycles.
EUHSD places a priority on professional learning and instructional material training essential for enhancing instructional
practices. The district has employed highly qualified Content Specialists in critical academic areas such as Math, English
Language Development, Humanities, and Science. Significant funding and human resources have been allocated to develop a
comprehensive arc of professional learning suitable for all classified, certificated, and management staff. Regular surveys and
facilitated listening sessions with staff and leadership from each content area are conducted in monthly subject-area council
meetings to ensure ongoing feedback and improvement.
Professional Learning Communities (PLCs) are integral to OGHS, where teachers engage in collective inquiry and action
research to improve student learning. These PLCs, organized by content area or course-alike teams, address essential
questions related to student learning expectations, assessments, interventions, and enrichment. Teams regularly examine
student work samples and data to ensure mastery of skills and standards, with a focus on short-term cycles of inquiry.
Restorative Practices and Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS) are emphasized through professional
development at OGHS. Teachers are trained in creating positive classroom cultures, establishing expectations, implementing
restorative responses to behavior, and fostering school-family communication.

Job-specific professional development is prioritized, with school counselors engaging in training appropriate to their roles,
