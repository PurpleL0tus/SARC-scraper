General Information about the School Accountability Report Card (SARC)
SARC Overview By February 1 of each year, every school in California is required by state law to
publish a School Accountability Report Card (SARC). The SARC contains
information about the condition and performance of each California public school.
Under the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) all local educational agencies
(LEAs) are required to prepare a Local Control and Accountability Plan (LCAP),
which describes how they intend to meet annual school-specific goals for all pupils,
with specific activities to address state and local priorities. Additionally, data
reported in an LCAP is to be consistent with data reported in the SARC.
- For more information about SARC requirements and access to prior year
reports, see the California Department of Education (CDE) SARC web page at
https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/ac/sa/.
- For more information about the LCFF or the LCAP, see the CDE LCFF web
page at https://www.cde.ca.gov/fg/aa/lc/.
- For additional information about the school, parents/guardians and community
members should contact the school principal or the district office.
DataQuest DataQuest is an online data tool located on the CDE DataQuest web page at
https://dq.cde.ca.gov/dataquest/ that contains additional information about this
school and comparisons of the school to the district and the county. Specifically,
DataQuest is a dynamic system that provides reports for accountability (e.g., test
data, enrollment, high school graduates, dropouts, course enrollments, staffing,
and data regarding English learners).
California School Dashboard The California School Dashboard (Dashboard)
https://www.caschooldashboard.org/ reflects California’s new accountability and
continuous improvement system and provides information about how LEAs and
schools are meeting the needs of California’s diverse student population. The
Dashboard contains reports that display the performance of LEAs, schools, and
student groups on a set of state and local measures to assist in identifying
strengths, challenges, and areas in need of improvement.
Internet Access Internet access is available at public libraries and other locations that are publicly
accessible (e.g., the California State Library). Access to the Internet at libraries and
public locations is generally provided on a first-come, first-served basis. Other use
restrictions may include the hours of operation, the length of time that a workstation
may be used (depending on availability), the types of software programs available
on a workstation, and the ability to print documents.

2023-24 School Description and Mission Statement
SCHOOL OVERVIEW:
Lemoore Online College Preparatory High School is a fully accredited online high school designed for students in grades nine
through twelve who are interested in a virtual/online classroom experience that promotes college and career readiness while on
the path to high school graduation. Our campus is located on the West Hills College campus in Lemoore, CA. Our unique
partnership with this community college allows us to streamline opportunities for "dual enrollment"; all Lemoore Online students
are encouraged to enroll in college courses wherein they earn both college credits while at the same completing the
requirements to earn a high school diploma.
OUR APPROACH:
As a 21st century school utilizing an entirely online curriculum, each of our students is issued a Chromebook so that they can
engage with the curriculum via the online learning platform - Edgenuity. The Lemoore Online teaching staff are all fully
credentialed in their respective content areas (e.g. Secondary math, English/ELD, AVID, science, social studies, PE, and
special education). Our staff has also completed additional staff development in order to enhance their ability to navigate the
online learning environment effectively in support of our independent study students with a focus on the following areas: 21st
century communication skills, virtual learning, digital tools, social-emotional learning, and trauma-informed practices. The
Lemoore Online staff takes great pride in our ability to foster supportive relationships between students, teachers, counselors,
administrators, and student advocates. We believe that each student's school experience functions best if it is supported by a
team of educational partners. At Lemoore Online each student is supported by a team consisting of: a teacher,
parents/guardians, a counselor, an administrator, etc., that work together to make up the student's "learning team". During
orientation, the Learning Team collaborates to create each student's individual academic plan which is re-evaluated during
weekly check-ins with the teacher (held in-person or virtually) that are used to both evaluate student progress and determine
student support needs. At Lemoore Online, the term “Independent Learner” doesn’t mean that the student is alone. Our staff
works with every student to make sure they are supported on their path to becoming a self-sufficient, independent learner. So
whether a student is focusing on credit recovery on their road to graduation or pursuing an accelerated, college prep program,
our teachers and staff get to know each student's goals and needs and use this knowledge to enhance the student learning
experience. Our teachers provide academic and emotional support that allows students to be successful while progressing at
their own pace. We understand that the goal of education is to encourage students to discover and develop their interests,

MISSION:

from a non-traditional school environment. Students will receive a standards and research-based college preparatory
curriculum in an online, virtual classroom experience along with supports from experienced teachers, counselors, and
administrators at our campus-based student support center.

All members of the Lemoore Online community will develop their mental, physical and emotional needs from an individualized
education that will aid them in achieving career and academic successes.

School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
School Facility Conditions and Planned Improvements
Lemoore Online College Preparatory High School staff and the Lemoore Union High School District take great efforts to ensure
that the school is clean, safe, and functional. To assist in this effort, the district utilizes a facility survey instrument developed by
the State of California OPSC. The results of the tool resulted in an overall "Exemplary" rating (100%) for the school facilities.
The results of this survey are available at the school office, at the LEA office, or on the Internet at:
lemooreonline.luhsd.k12.ca.us.
Below is more specific information on the condition of the school and the efforts made to ensure that students are provided with
a clean, safe, and functional learning environment.
This school consists of the following: there are 2 classrooms (i.e. The Learning Center) utilized by all students and teachers;
there is 1 "Conference Room" utilized by Student Services and the Special Education teacher; there is 1 Counselor’s office,
and 1 Administrative office. The main campus, located on the West Hills College campus in Lemoore, CA, consists of portable
buildings and is seventeen years old. In conjunction with Lemoore Middle College High School (and the greater Lemoore Union
High School District), Lemoore Online shares restroom and food service facilities. All buildings have had the roofing replaced
within the last 4 years. New filtered water fountains were installed recently in the cafeteria and in one of the classrooms for
student and staff use. The Lemoore Online "Learning Center" buildings were remodeled 3 years ago in order to increase the
square footage to accommodate the growing student population. District maintenance staff ensures that the repairs necessary
to keep the school in good repair and working order are completed in a timely manner. A work order process is used to ensure
efficient service and that emergency repairs are given the highest priority.

2023-24 School Safety Plan
2023-24 School Safety Plan
Lemoore Online College Preparatory High School, as part of the LUHSD, has a School Safety Plan and Crisis Intervention Plan
on file and available for parents as well as the general public. The school plans are reviewed and updated on an annual basis.
The plan has been developed by the staff with input from parents and community members to work to ensure a safe and non-
violent environment. The School Safety Plan also includes information on communication with outside organizations, police,
and fire protection. Meetings are held regularly with the Lemoore Police Department, Probation Office and the Sheriff’s Office to
make sure that the lines of communication are open and all those involved know their role.
Additionally, practice drills are held annually to make sure that students and staff understand what to do and where to go in the
event of different types of emergency situations. The district also provides a Youth Development Officer who is shared with
other schools in the district. A campus monitor is shared between Lemoore Middle College High School and Lemoore Online to
provide constant supervision of the school grounds and students.

Professional Development
Professional Development
Professional Development is a crucial part of our commitment to continuous improvement at Lemoore Online. Throughout the
course of the school year this work takes on various forms. Over the summer, several of our staff members (including our
principal and some of our teachers) attended AVID (Advancement Via Individual Determination) training via an online AVID
Conference. This work is directly correlated to our Homeroom class (a core element of our program) where each student has
weekly engagement with topics such as: time management, work ethic, academic language, college readiness, goal setting,
etc. As a District, we have also made a commitment to Equity training with our entire staff. At Lemoore Online we tailored this
work to fit the needs of both our students and our staff by exploring topics such as: social-emotional learning, trauma-informed
practices, self-care for educators, bias, and modifying assessments to accommodate diverse learning needs. During our
monthly Staff Meeting we are continuously exploring more effective ways to support, guide, mentor, intervene with, and
encourage our students. During Staff Meetings the principal reviews current student data in several key areas (e.g. student
attendance, grades, behavior, engagement in Homeroom, and the MTSS process) and the staff work on a plan that addresses
current needs according to the data. Finally, our staff is developing an ELD Program to begin in the 24-25 school year. In
anticipation, two of our teachers have been working with the Kings County Office of Education developing a Designated ELD
class which will support the language acquisition needs of our English Learner students.-------
